\begin{abstract}
Voblint formalizes an executable interprocedural static analysis pipeline in
Isabelle/HOL, following Goblint's D/G architecture. The development connects
programs in VIMP, a procedural imperative language, to sound abstract results
through a verified control-flow graph compiler, an equation generator, and a
verified side-effecting top-down solver. The soundness argument applies to the
result computed by the analyzer.

The framework separates program semantics, abstract domains, transfer
operations, and solver integration. Its D/G interface distinguishes local
analysis states from shared information communicated through side effects.
Reusable soundness results support context-insensitive and context-sensitive
analyses. Concrete instances include sign, interval, parity, and congruence
analyses, together with a reduced product of these four domains.

The main soundness theorem relates every finite source-execution prefix to a
corresponding control-flow graph state, establishes coverage by the computed
abstract result, and proves the correctness of reported check verdicts. It
applies to accepted programs and configurations under a solver-termination
assumption; source-program termination is unnecessary. The verified pipeline
begins with a VIMP abstract syntax tree. Parsing, code generation, and the
external compilation and runtime toolchain remain outside the proved
guarantees. This document presents the formalization by session, together with
an executable end-to-end certificate and the code-export boundary.
\end{abstract}
