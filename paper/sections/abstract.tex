\begin{abstract}
Goblint-style analyzers solve side-effecting equations, where evaluating one
unknown can contribute to other unknowns. An existing Isabelle/HOL proof establishes solver partial correctness
without connecting the equations to execution. We mechanize this connection
for an integer language with recursive procedures, without pointers or heap.

We adapt the local traces of Schwarz et al.\ to record procedure activations
and the callers resumed by returns. A context policy relates concrete calls
to possibly several analysis contexts. A cover assigns candidate sets of reachable stores to program points and contexts.
We prove that five local obligations suffice for a cover to contain every
valid trace's final store in every admitted context, for any control-flow
graph. With sound abstract operations and compatible context routing,
post-solutions (assignments of abstract values satisfying the inequalities,
including side effects) yield such covers. The verified solver supplies them.

For every accepted configuration, when the analyzer function exported to
OCaml returns a report, it overapproximates the stores reached by finite
source executions from the prescribed initial states. The report's definite verdicts
correctly classify check conditions as true or false whenever reached;
neither verdict asserts reachability. The theorem establishes partial correctness of the
Isabelle function; solver termination is not proved, and parsing and the
code-generation toolchain remain outside the proof.
\end{abstract}
