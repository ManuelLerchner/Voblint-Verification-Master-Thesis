\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

A machine-checked theorem can hold for uninteresting reasons. Its premises may
never hold together, its obligations may exclude no error a real analyzer
makes, and an analyzer that answers \verdict{UNKNOWN} everywhere satisfies it
as well (\isaname{unknown_everywhere_sound}). We therefore check that the
premises can hold and that the obligations have force, and then measure what
contexts and domains gain in precision on concrete programs. Each item states its kind of
evidence. Facts about one fixed program are proved by \texttt{eval}, which runs
generated code and therefore also trusts the code generator. Analyzer outputs
are registered claims that \texttt{pixi run thesis-claims} re-checks;
\cref{tab:contexts,tab:costs} are generated from claims and from the committed
profiling data.

\subparagraph*{The theorem is not vacuous (machine-checked, one program each).}
For the program of \cref{fig:running}, analyzed with Interval, entry-state
contexts, and the warrowing update rule, a theory builds a source execution
that returns from both calls. Evaluation shows that the analyzer answers with
both checks \verdict{PROVED}, and \isaname{nv_source_certified} instantiates
\cref{thm:headline} with every premise discharged. Companion instances do the
same for a single check (\isaname{nv_check_proved_sound}) and for a
\verdict{DEAD} check (\isaname{nv_dead_unreached}). They show that the premises are satisfiable.

\subparagraph*{The obligations have force (machine-checked).}
\oblig{Total} is needed: \isaname{total_dropped_unsound} exhibits a cover that
meets the other four obligations and misses a reached store. At the domain
level, Goblint once claimed \texttt{c \% 2 == 1} for $c \in \{-5, -7\}$,
although C's truncating remainder gives $-1$ (issue 1156, fixed
in~\cite{goblint1161}). The defect is in a domain, not in contexts. Every Voblint
domain's remainder must meet \isaname{mod_sound} of the locale
\isaname{sound_arith_ops} (the truncating remainder of concrete values lies in
the concretization of the abstract one), which the shipped congruence
remainder discharges by \isaname{congruence_mod_sound}. \isaname{prefix_congruence_mod_unsound} proves
that every abstract remainder returning the constant~$1$ for
$(1 + 2\mathbb{Z}) \bmod 2$ violates the obligation at $-5$, so the defective
operator could not be registered.

\subparagraph*{Contexts decide checks on recursive programs (executable).}
\Cref{tab:contexts} runs one regression program under every context policy.
Mutually recursive procedures \texttt{f} and \texttt{g} return $2$ and $1$ at
the bottom of the recursion. \texttt{main} calls \texttt{f(2)} and
\texttt{g(2)} inside a loop of 200 iterations and checks both results. Each
call chain is three call sites deep. The table also counts the copies of
\texttt{f} and \texttt{g} the analysis creates. With one context per
procedure, or with call strings of length 1, the activations of different
depths share a state and both results widen to $[1,12]$. With call strings of length 2 or with
entry-state contexts, the depths stay apart and both checks are proved. The
loop adds no contexts, because the callee contexts do not depend on the loop
counter. For a related program, the theories prove that the solution of the
routed equation system at call-string length 2 is strictly more precise at the
entry of a callee than at length 1
(\isaname{sign_k2_strictly_more_precise_than_k1_at_g}). That theorem compares
two solutions computed by \texttt{eval}. It concerns neither
\isaname{run_voblint} nor call strings in general.

\subparagraph*{Running time (empirical).}
Speed was not a goal: the generated code runs as Isabelle emits it, plus one
proved code equation. The profiling run at repository revision
\texttt{17fcb363d} analyzed the 380 regression fixtures that set no timeout of
their own under 15 configurations: five domains times three context
policies (none, call strings of length 1, entry states). Eleven fixtures are
ill-formed programs and are excluded, which leaves \stat{prof.fixtures}
fixtures and \stat{prof.cases} cases. A case's time is the median of three repetitions on an
Apple M4 Pro; a case that times out (30\,s) or fails in any repetition counts
as failed. \Cref{tab:costs} summarizes the successful cases
per policy. All \stat{prof.timeouts} timeouts are entry-state cases with
Interval, Int or Congruence on seven recursive fixtures, where each recursion
level creates a new context. One is
\texttt{24-site-figures/precision/13-recursion\_grows\_warrow.vimp}: \texttt{f(x)}
checks \texttt{x >= 0} and calls \texttt{f(x + 1)}. It is proved without contexts and
does not terminate with entry-state contexts. Call strings of length 2 were
not profiled. On generated programs of growing size, solve time
grows with fitted exponents of \stat{prof.scaling.time_min} to
\stat{prof.scaling.time_max} while solver evaluations grow about linearly
(\cref{sec:mechanization}). These measurements cover small programs on one
machine and imply no performance bound.
