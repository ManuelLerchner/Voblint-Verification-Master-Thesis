\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Activation traces give calling contexts a concrete counterpart, and with it
the soundness of a Goblint-style analyzer reduces to five local obligations
that a solved, context-routed equation system meets. The resulting theorem
covers the analyzer function that is exported and run, for every configuration
it offers.

The result has limits we have stated where they apply and collect here. It is
partial correctness, since the solver's termination is a per-program fact
established by running it. VIMP is scalar: it has no pointers, arrays, heap, or
threads, and the adequacy of its semantics is argued, not proved. No precision
or completeness property is proved.

Several results do not depend on the details of VIMP: the solver certificate
is stated over unknowns and right-hand sides, and the coverage contract
refers to the language only through a few step functions. Features that change
only these step functions, such as machine integers, leave the contract and
its composition intact. The trace semantics, however, assumes that a callee
cannot modify its caller's locals, so a return can resume the caller with the
locals it had before the call. Pointers into the caller's frame, a shared heap,
or threads break this assumption and would require extending the trace
semantics itself.
