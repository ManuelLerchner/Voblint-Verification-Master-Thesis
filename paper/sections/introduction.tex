\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A context-sensitive static analyzer can distinguish abstract states for
different calling contexts at the same program point. A context groups calls,
for example by their call sites or abstract argument values. In the program of \cref{fig:running},
\texttt{main} calls \texttt{bump(5)} and \texttt{bump(4)}, and \texttt{bump}
returns its argument plus one. An interval analysis without calling contexts must describe both calls at
the return of \texttt{bump} and therefore cannot prove either check. With contexts distinguishing the arguments, the analysis handles each call
separately and proves both checks. Goblint analyzes C~\cite{vojdani16,seidl26} using such contexts through a
\emph{side-effecting constraint system}~\cite{seidl03,apinis12}. Its inequations relate unknowns
indexed by program point and context, and the right-hand side of one unknown
may also contribute to other unknowns. A generic top-down solver starts from
a queried unknown and discovers dependencies as it evaluates right-hand sides.
A defect in any of these layers can produce an unsound verdict.
\Cref{sec:evaluation} examines one such defect in Goblint's remainder operation.

Stade et al.\ proved the solver partially correct in
Isabelle/HOL~\cite{stade24}, and Tilscher et al.\ extended the proof to side
effects~\cite{tilscher26}. A valuation assigns an abstract value to each unknown.
When the solver terminates, its valuation satisfies
the inequations for all solved unknowns. It is a \emph{post-solution} on that set. The solved set is closed under right-hand-side reads of local unknowns:
reading a local unknown requires it to be solved too. These properties form
the solver's certificate. To derive program correctness from it, we must prove that the
equations soundly approximate execution and that the report preserves their
guarantee. The semantic connection requires associating reachable stores
(assignments of integer values to variables) with calling contexts. Such semantics are established in the
literature~\cite{sharir81,knoop92,jeannet04,vanhorn10} (\cref{sec:related}). To
our knowledge, no mechanization connects a source semantics through such a context-indexed
semantics to a side-effecting constraint system and its verified solver.

We mechanize this connection in Voblint (verified Goblint), an Isabelle/HOL
formalization.
\begin{itemize}
  \item We adapt the local traces of Schwarz et al.~\cite{schwarz21} to
    procedure activations, each representing one invocation of a procedure. A context policy, which may admit several contexts
    for one call, relates these activation traces to contexts. A candidate overapproximation, or \emph{cover}, assigns sets of stores to
    nodes and contexts. We give five local obligations on covers for arbitrary
    control-flow graphs. We prove that any cover satisfying them contains the final store of every valid trace
    in every admitted context (\cref{sec:traces}).
  \item We prove that every post-solution of the generated, context-routed
    equations meets these obligations, assuming sound abstract operations and
    agreement between the context policy and the routing that selects callee contexts. The verified solver enters only through its
    certificate (\cref{sec:equations}).
  \item We prove an end-to-end theorem about the analyzer function that
    Isabelle exports to OCaml. For every accepted configuration, whenever the
    analyzer returns a report, the report covers every store reached by a finite source
    execution at its program point. Every definite verdict correctly classifies
    a check as true or false whenever execution reaches it (\cref{sec:theorem}).
  \item We exhibit concrete instances of the theorem, show that the faulty
    remainder operation behind a Goblint defect violates a domain obligation,
    and compare the verdicts obtained with different calling contexts on
    recursive programs (\cref{sec:evaluation}).
\end{itemize}
The solver, constraint systems, local traces, and separation of analyses and
solvers are inherited. Our context-indexed collecting semantics groups
reachable stores by node and context. The report guarantees cover both
individual contexts and their union at each node (\cref{sec:theorem}).

VIMP supports local and global integer variables and recursive procedures with
parameters and return values, but no pointers, arrays, or heap. The correctness
results are conditional on solver termination, which is not proved for every
program. The parser and report printer are unverified handwritten code;
Isabelle's code generator, the OCaml toolchain, and the solver-tracing hook
used in traced runs are trusted.
