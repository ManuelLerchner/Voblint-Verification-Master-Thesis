\section{From Equations to a Cover}
\label{sec:equations}

The contract of \cref{sec:traces} asks for a cover. This section constructs one
from a solved equation system. The ingredients have different origins. The
analysis interface mirrors Goblint's \texttt{Spec}~\cite{vojdani16,seidl26}.
Side-effecting equations come from Seidl et al.\ and Apinis
et al.~\cite{seidl03,apinis12}. The solver and its certificate are those of
Tilscher et al.~\cite{tilscher26}. The routing conditions, which connect the
equations to the context policy, and their discharge for each policy are ours.

\subsection{What an analysis supplies}

An analysis describes the stores at a node by an abstract state $d$. Its
meaning $\conc(d, \rho)$ also depends on $\rho$, the solved values of the
analysis's global unknowns. We suppress $\rho$ when it plays no role. The
analysis supplies the record \isaname{dg_spec}, the counterpart of Goblint's
\texttt{Spec}: one transfer per kind of edge action, an enter operation, and
two combine operations. A call needs two states. After the return, the
caller's locals come from before the call, while the globals and the result
come from the callee. Enter therefore returns a list of entry pairs $(q, d)$:
a resume state $q$ describing the caller's store and an entry state $d$
describing the store in which the callee starts. After the return,
\emph{combine\_env} merges the callee's exit state into $q$, taking the
callee's globals, and \emph{combine\_assign} then writes the return value to
the call's destination. Together they mirror the concrete
$\mathit{combine}_e$ of \cref{sec:traces}. A transfer may also publish to
the analysis's global unknowns, and its output is read in the previous global
values joined with what it publishes.

The local soundness laws of transfers and combine form the locale
\isaname{analysis_contract}: the concretization is monotone, every transfer
loses no successor store, and combine covers $\mathit{combine}_e(s, t)$
whenever $q$ covers $s$ and the exit state covers $t$. Enter is not part of
that locale. That enter returns a pair covering the caller's store and the
entered store (\isaname{entry_pairs_cover}) is used by the routing conditions
below, which each context policy discharges. A numeric
domain proves only its abstract operations sound. The framework derives the
transfers once for all domains, following Nipkow and Klein's generic
interpreter~\cite[Sect.~13.5]{nipkow14}. The shipped domains are Sign,
Interval, Parity, Congruence, and their reduced product Int.

\subsection{Context-indexed equations and routing}

For every node $v$ and context $c$ there is a local unknown $(v, c)$. Its
right-hand side joins four sources (\isaname{routed_node_rhs_parts}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item the initial state at the program entry;
  \item the transfer along each intraprocedural edge into $v$, in the same
    context $c$;
  \item the combined result of each call returning to $v$;
  \item at a procedure entry, the entry states published to it.
\end{itemize}
At a call, a \emph{routing function} $\mathit{route}$ chooses the callee
context from the call site, the caller context, the entry state and the call.
For a call edge $(u, e, \ctor{FunctionEntry}\;p, k)$ and caller context $c$,
the right-hand side of the continuation $(k, c)$ contains, for every entry pair
$(q, d)$ that enter returns for $\sol(u, c)$
(\isaname{routed_call_alternative_program}): $\mathit{combine}(q, \bot)$ if $d$
is bottom, and otherwise, with $c' = \mathit{route}(u, c, d, e)$, the program
\[
\text{publish } d \text{ to } \mathit{seed}(p, c');\quad
r \leftarrow \text{read } (\ctor{FunctionResult}\;p, c');\quad
\mathit{combine}(q, r).
\]
The entry equation of $(\ctor{FunctionEntry}\;p, c')$ reads the global unknown
$\mathit{seed}(p, c')$. In the running example, the call \texttt{bump(5)} in
$c_0$ publishes $n \in [5,5]$ to $\mathit{seed}(\texttt{bump}, [5,5])$, reads
the result of \texttt{bump} in $[5,5]$, and combines it with the resume state
of \texttt{main}. A recursive call needs no special treatment: its equation
reads a result that depends on the caller's own unknowns, and the solver
handles the cycle as it handles a loop.

The equations choose a callee context from an abstract state, while the
concrete semantics chooses one from the concrete call through the policy
$\adm$. Two conditions make the choices agree. Both are assumptions of the
locale \isaname{routed_context}.
\begin{description}
  \item[Adequacy] (\isaname{routing_adequate}). If $\adm$ admits $c'$ for a call
    from a store that the caller's solved state describes, then enter produces
    a pair $(q, d)$ routed to $c'$. The pair's resume state covers the
    caller's store, and its entry state covers the entered store. The
    callee's entry unknown in $c'$ is solved.
  \item[Totality] (\isaname{routing_total}). $\adm$ admits at least one context
    for every call from a store that the caller's solved state describes.
\end{description}
For the unit policy and for call strings, $\adm$ admits exactly the context the
routing function computes from the call site and the caller context. Totality
is then immediate, and adequacy reduces to entry coverage and the routed entry
unknown being solved.

For entry-state contexts, the policy \isaname{routed_entry_context_rel} is
defined from the solved valuation $\sol$:
\begin{multline*}
\adm_{u,c}(e, s, s') \;=\; \{\, \mathit{route}(u, c, d, e) \mid {}\\
(q, d) \in \mathit{enter}_e(\sol(u, c)),\; s \in \conc(q),\; s' \in \conc(d) \,\}.
\end{multline*}
In words, a context is admitted when an entry pair of the solved caller state
covers both concrete stores and is routed there. The definition is not
circular. $\sol$ is fixed before the policy is applied, and the final
guarantee concerns $\NodeColl$, which no policy changes. The policy only
decides how $\NodeColl$ is split among contexts inside the proof. \Cref{sec:related} compares this with a posteriori
soundness~\cite{might09}. A consequence
matters for reading the result: under entry-state contexts, $\ActColl(v, c)$
is relative to the analysis that produced $\sol$.

\begin{theorem}[Routed collecting soundness, \isaname{activation_collect_dg_sound}]
\label{thm:routed}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{tab:hypotheses}, if $(\mathit{entry}_g, c_0) \in W$,
the initial state describes the initial stores under initial global values
$\rho_0$, and $\rho_0 \leq \rho$, then $\ActColl(v, c) \subseteq \cover(v, c)$
for all $v$ and $c$, where $\cover(v, c) = \conc(\sol(v, c), \rho)$ on $W$
and $\emptyset$ elsewhere.
\end{theorem}

The proof checks the five obligations of \cref{thm:contract} for this cover.
The initial state and $\rho_0 \leq \rho$ give \oblig{Init}. The edge
transfers and \texttt{fwd} give \oblig{Intra}. Adequacy with entry coverage
gives \oblig{Call}, adequacy with combine soundness gives \oblig{Return}, and
totality gives \oblig{Total}. Both bounds of \texttt{pp} are used: a value
reaches its target unknown either as an answer or as a published side effect,
such as an entry state published to a seed.
\oblig{Return} is quantified over every admitted call edge from the call site
(\cref{sec:traces}), and \texttt{calls\_unique} reduces it to the edge being
returned through.

\subsection{The solver enters through one certificate}

\Cref{thm:routed} needs a set $W$ of unknowns that contains the program entry,
is closed under execution steps, and on which $\sol$ is bounded. The verified top-down
solver~\cite{tilscher26} provides a different certificate,
\isaname{part_post_solution}, for the query $x$ it started on, its solved set
$V$, and its result $\sol$: the query is in $V$, $V$ is closed under the reads of right-hand sides, and $\sol$ bounds
both the right-hand side and every side effect of each solved unknown. The two certificates point in different
directions. The solver works backward from its query, the result of
\texttt{main}, while \cref{thm:routed} follows executions forward from the
entry. The bridge takes $W$ to be the \emph{live} part of $V$: the unknowns $(u, c)$
such that $u$ belongs to a procedure $q$ whose result
$(\ctor{FunctionResult}\;q, c)$ is in $V$ (\isaname{live_unknowns}). The
compiler ends every body with a return edge, so every node of a procedure
reaches the procedure's result through intraprocedural edges and
call-to-continuation steps. The result's right-hand side reads its
predecessors, so closure under reads puts all nodes of a procedure into $V$
once its result is in $V$ in that context. Calls carry this into callees. A
continuation $(k, c)$ in $V$ reads the callee's result
$(\ctor{FunctionResult}\;p, c')$ whenever the routed entry state is not bottom
(\isaname{sol_vars_callee_result}), so the callee's nodes are live in $c'$,
its entry among them. For a bottom entry state, no store enters the callee and
nothing is needed. \isaname{live_unknowns_cover} proves, from a well-formed
program and a returned solve, that the live set contains the program entry
and satisfies the closure hypotheses of \cref{tab:hypotheses}. This is
structural reachability in the graph. It holds also for an activation that
never returns, because the argument follows the equations, not executions.
Three sets are involved: the solver's solved set $V$, its live subset $W$, on
which \cref{thm:routed} is applied, and the states the report publishes,
which are taken from $V$. Publishing more
states than the proof uses is sound, because the report only has to contain
the cover.

The rest of the proof never inspects the solver. The analysis locale takes the
solver as a parameter and assumes four facts about it
(\isaname{certified_solver}): a run in its domain yields
\isaname{part_post_solution} and a finite solved set, and a run of its
executable form that returns lies in the domain and agrees with the logical
solver. These facts are proved for the vendored solver under each of its five
update rules for global unknowns (\isaname{td_certified_solver}). An update
rule decides how contributions to a global unknown accumulate: by join, by
join per contributing right-hand side, or by \emph{warrowing}, which combines
widening and narrowing~\cite{tilscher26}. Replacing the solver would not touch
the semantic layer.
