\section{From Equations to a Cover}
\label{sec:equations}

The contract of \cref{sec:traces} asks for a cover. This section constructs one
from a solved equation system. The goal is that every post-solution $\sol$ of
the generated equations satisfies
$\ActColl(v, c) \subseteq \conc(\sol(v, c))$ at every solved node and context.
The construction has three seams, each with its own proof obligations: what an
analysis supplies, how contexts are routed, and what the solver guarantees
(\cref{fig:seams}).

\subsection{What an analysis supplies}

An analysis describes the stores at a program point by an abstract state $d$
and supplies the record \isaname{dg_spec}, the counterpart of Goblint's
\texttt{Spec} interface: one transfer per kind of edge action, an
\emph{enter} operation, and two \emph{combine} operations. A call needs two
states, because after the return the caller's locals come from before the
call, while the globals and the result come from the callee. Enter therefore
returns a list of pairs of a \emph{resume state}~$q$, describing the caller's
store, and an \emph{entry state}~$e$, describing the store the callee starts
with. Combine builds the state after the call from $q$ and the callee's exit
state. The local soundness laws are collected in the locale
\isaname{analysis_contract}: the concretization $\conc$ is monotone, every
transfer loses no successor store, and combine covers $\mathit{combine}(s, t)$
whenever $q$ covers $s$ and the exit state covers $t$. Enter must return a pair
covering the caller's store and the entered store
(\isaname{entry_pairs_cover}).

A numeric domain does not prove these laws itself. It implements abstract
arithmetic, comparisons, and guard refinement with their soundness laws, and
the framework derives the transfers and proves them sound once for all domains,
following Nipkow and Klein's generic interpreter~\cite[Sect.~13.5]{nipkow14}.
The shipped domains are Sign, Interval, Parity, Congruence, and their reduced
product. None of these proofs mentions a context, a routing function, or a
solver.

\subsection{Context-indexed equations with routing}

For every node $v$ and context $c$ there is a local unknown $(v, c)$. Its
right-hand side joins four sources: the initial state at the program entry,
the transfer along each local edge into $v$ in the same context $c$, the
combined result of each call returning to $v$, and, at a procedure entry, the
entry states published to it (\isaname{routed_node_rhs_parts}). At a call, a
\emph{routing function} chooses the callee context from the call site, the
caller context, and the entry state. Under entry-state contexts the choice
depends on a state that is known only during solving, so the callee cannot
list its callers in advance. Each call therefore publishes its entry state as
a side effect to a global seed unknown for the callee and context, which the
callee's entry equation reads. A recursive call needs no special treatment:
its equation reads an exit state that depends on the caller's own unknowns, and
the solver handles the cycle as it handles a loop.

The equations choose a callee context from an abstract state; the concrete
semantics chooses one from the concrete call through the policy~$\adm$. The
two must agree. \emph{Adequacy} (\isaname{routing_adequate}) requires that
whenever $\adm$ admits $c'$ for a call from a store the caller's solved state
describes, some entry pair covering the call is routed and published to $c'$.
\emph{Totality} (\isaname{routing_total}) is \oblig{Total} for the generated
equations. For the unit policy and for call strings, $\adm$ admits exactly the
context the routing function computes, so totality is immediate, and adequacy reduces to the entry coverage of enter
and the routed entry unknown being solved. For
entry-state contexts, $\adm$ is defined from the routing itself: it admits the
context that an entry pair of the solved caller state covering the call is
routed to. The policy may depend on the analysis result without circularity,
because the final guarantee concerns $\NodeColl$, which no policy changes.

\begin{theorem}[Routed collecting soundness, \isaname{activation_collect_dg_sound}]
\label{thm:routed}
Let $\sol$ be a post-solution on a set of unknowns that contains the program
entry in context $c_0$ and is closed under local edges, call continuations, and
routed calls. Assume the analysis contract, adequate and total routing, side conditions on
call resolution and the shape of the graph, and that the initial state
describes the initial stores. Then
$\ActColl(v, c) \subseteq \cover(v, c)$, where $\cover(v, c)$ is
$\conc(\sol(v, c))$ on solved unknowns and empty elsewhere.
\end{theorem}

The proof checks the five obligations for this cover, one source of the
right-hand side each: the initial state gives \oblig{Init}, edge transfers give
\oblig{Intra}, adequacy gives \oblig{Call} and \oblig{Return}, and totality
gives \oblig{Total}. \Cref{thm:contract} then yields the inclusion. The theorem
holds for every domain and every context policy that proves adequacy and
totality.

\subsection{The solver enters through one certificate}

\Cref{thm:routed} needs a post-solution on a closed set of unknowns. The
verified top-down solver~\cite{tilscher26} provides the certificate
\isaname{part_post_solution} for the query $x$ it was started on, its solved
set $V$, and its result $\sol$:
\snippet{part_post_solution}
\noindent The query is solved, the solved set is closed under the reads of
right-hand sides, and $\sol$ bounds both the right-hand side and every side
effect of each solved unknown. Two directions have to meet. The solver works
backward from its query, the result of \texttt{main}, while
\cref{thm:routed} follows executions forward from the entry. Every node of a
procedure reaches its result node, because the compiler ends every body with a
return edge, so closure under reads makes all nodes of a procedure solved in a
context once its result is. \isaname{live_unknowns_cover} proves the forward
closure from a well-formed program and a returned solve.

The rest of the proof never inspects the solver. The analysis locale takes the
solver as a parameter and assumes four facts about it
(\isaname{certified_solver}): a run in its domain yields
\isaname{part_post_solution} and a finite solved set, and a run of its
executable form that returns lies in the domain and agrees with the logical
solver. These facts are proved once for the vendored solver under each of its
five update rules for global unknowns (\isaname{td_certified_solver}).
Replacing the solver would not touch the semantic layer.
