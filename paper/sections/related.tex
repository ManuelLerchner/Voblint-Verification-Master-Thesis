\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subparagraph*{Concrete semantics of calling contexts.}
Sharir and Pnueli distinguish the functional approach,
which computes input--output relations of procedures, from the call-string
approach, which tags information with the history of calls~\cite{sharir81}.
Voblint's call-string policy follows the second, and its entry-state policy,
which indexes a callee's exit states by its entry state, resembles a
functional approach tabulated per input. Knoop and Steffen handle the locals of recursive procedures with
stacks of lattice elements, of which the maximal-fixpoint strategy needs only
two~\cite{knoop92}; Voblint's return has the shape of their return
function~\cite[§4.1]{knoop92}. Reps et al.\ solve such
problems precisely when facts are finite and transfer functions
distributive~\cite{reps95}. Voblint requires neither and proves no precision
result. Jeannet and Serwe derive interprocedural analyses by abstract
interpretation of a standard stack-based operational semantics and combine the
call-string and functional approaches~\cite{jeannet04}. Van Horn and Might
derive sound analyses from abstract machines by bounding their
stores~\cite{vanhorn10}. These give contexts a concrete counterpart on paper. In the a
posteriori soundness of Might and Manolios~\cite{might09}, the state
abstraction is fixed in advance and a map from concrete to abstract contexts
is chosen per run, after the analysis, from non-empty candidate sets. Voblint's entry-state policy instead reads a single relation off the
solution, with \oblig{Total} in the role of non-emptiness
(\cref{sec:equations}).

\subparagraph*{Verified analyzers.}
Verasco is a Coq-verified analyzer for most of C99, excluding recursion and
dynamic allocation~\cite{jourdan15}. Its interpreter iterates over the
structure of C\#minor, reanalyzes a function at every call site, and raises an
alarm at a possible recursive call. Blazy et al.\ check the candidate of an untrusted iterator with a verified
checker~\cite{blazy13}. Nipkow and Klein develop
abstract interpretation for IMP in Isabelle/HOL~\cite{nipkow12,nipkow14}, and
Voblint's non-relational domains follow their generic interpreter. Their
language has no procedures. Franceschino et al.~\cite{franceschino21} and Cachera and
Pichardie~\cite{cachera10} verify executable analyzers for languages without
procedures.
Michelland et al.\ make soundness proofs reusable by sharing one interpreter
between the concrete and the abstract semantics~\cite{michelland24}. Cachera
et al.\ compute a context-insensitive dataflow analysis with a verified solver
for ordinary inequations~\cite{cachera05}.

\subparagraph*{Contexts in mechanized semantics.}
Dabrowski and Pichardie instrument a concrete semantics with contexts through
a module type whose call-context function computes a callee's context, and
prove a context-sensitive points-to analysis sound in Coq~\cite{dabrowski09}.
This is the closest precedent for Voblint's context-indexed semantics. Their
analyses are given only as constraints that no solver
computes~\cite[§7]{dabrowski09}. Voblint's
contexts are admitted by a relation, the totality condition makes the indexed
collection exhaustive, and the indexing is connected to an executable verified
solver. Trace partitioning indexes sets of traces by control
history~\cite{rival07}; Rival and Mauborgne allow coverings in which one trace
has several indices and present calling contexts as one such indexing. The
activation collecting semantics is a covering of this relational kind. Among
Isabelle developments, Lammich and Müller-Olm verify the soundness and
precision of a conflict analysis for programs with procedures and
threads~\cite{lammich07afp}, Wasserrab verifies an interprocedural
slicer~\cite{wasserrab09afp}, and Breitner formalizes Shivers' control-flow
analysis for functional programs~\cite{breitner10afp}. None of these entries
claims an extracted analyzer or represents an activation with its caller
chain.

\subparagraph*{Verified solvers.}
Hofmann et al.\ verify a local generic solver in Coq~\cite{hofmann10}, and de
Vilhena et al.\ verify one that discovers dependencies dynamically in
Iris~\cite{devilhena20}. Stade et al.\ prove the top-down solver partially
correct in Isabelle/HOL~\cite{stade24}, and Tilscher et al.\ extend it to side
effects with a generic interface for update rules~\cite{tilscher26}. Voblint
vendors the latter. Their termination result for top-down solvers without
side effects assumes a finite type of unknowns~\cite{tilscher26jar}, which
Voblint's node and context types are not.

\subparagraph*{Goblint and local traces.}
Side-effecting constraint systems~\cite{seidl03,apinis12} and the split
between analyses and solvers are Goblint's architecture~\cite{vojdani16,seidl26},
and Voblint's \isaname{dg_spec} follows its \texttt{Spec} interface. No
theorem transfers to Goblint's OCaml implementation. Schwarz et al.\ prove
Goblint's thread-modular analyses sound against local traces on
paper~\cite{schwarz21,schwarz23}; activation traces adapt the construction,
but no concurrency result transfers.

\subparagraph*{Differences from prior work.}
Voblint inherits its solver, the side-effecting constraint systems, the
analysis interface, the shape of its traces, and the distinction between
call-string and functional contexts. Voblint adds their mechanized
connection, ending in a theorem about the exported analyzer; we know of no
prior mechanization of it. Dabrowski and Pichardie~\cite{dabrowski09} have
contexts in the semantics but no solver. Verasco~\cite{jourdan15} covers a far
larger language but reanalyzes functions per call site, raises an alarm at recursion,
and iterates structurally instead of using a generic solver.
