\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subparagraph*{Concrete semantics of calling contexts.}
How information about a call reaches the callee and returns to the caller is a
classical question. Sharir and Pnueli distinguish the functional approach,
which computes input--output relations of procedures, from the call-string
approach, which tags information with the history of calls~\cite{sharir81}.
Voblint's call-string policy follows the second, and its entry-state policy,
which indexes a callee's exit states by its entry state, resembles a
functional approach tabulated per input. The return combines the caller's
state before the call with the callee's exit state, the shape of the return
function of Knoop and Steffen~\cite[§4.1]{knoop92}. Reps et al.\ solve such
problems precisely when facts are finite and transfer functions
distributive~\cite{reps95}. Voblint requires neither and proves no precision
result. Jeannet and Serwe derive interprocedural analyses by abstract
interpretation of a standard stack-based operational semantics and combine the
call-string and functional approaches~\cite{jeannet04}. Van Horn and Might
derive sound analyses from abstract machines by bounding their
stores~\cite{vanhorn10}. These works give contexts a concrete counterpart on
paper. Voblint mechanizes one such counterpart and connects it to equations
and a verified solver. Might and Manolios introduce a posteriori soundness,
whose abstraction maps are constructed after the analysis has
run~\cite{might09}. Voblint's entry-state policy is similar in spirit: the
abstraction is fixed in advance, but the relation between traces and contexts
is read off the solution (\cref{sec:equations}).

\subparagraph*{Verified analyzers.}
Verasco is a Coq-verified analyzer for most of C99, excluding recursion and
dynamic allocation~\cite{jourdan15}. Its interpreter iterates over the
structure of C\#minor, reanalyzes a function at every call site, and raises an
alarm at a possible recursive call. The value analysis of Blazy et al.\ checks the candidate of an
untrusted iterator with a verified checker~\cite{blazy13}; Voblint instead runs
a solver whose partial correctness is proved. Nipkow and Klein develop
abstract interpretation for IMP in Isabelle/HOL~\cite{nipkow12,nipkow14}, and
Voblint's non-relational domains follow their generic interpreter. Their
language has no procedures. Franceschino et al.\ verify an IMP analyzer in F*
that runs in a browser~\cite{franceschino21}, and Cachera and Pichardie extract
a certified denotational interpreter~\cite{cachera10}; neither has procedures.
Michelland et al.\ make soundness proofs reusable by sharing one interpreter
between the concrete and the abstract semantics~\cite{michelland24}. Cachera
et al.\ compute a context-insensitive dataflow analysis with a verified solver
for ordinary inequations~\cite{cachera05}.

\subparagraph*{Contexts in mechanized semantics.}
Dabrowski and Pichardie instrument a concrete semantics with contexts through
a module type whose call-context function computes a callee's context, and
prove a context-sensitive points-to analysis sound in Coq~\cite{dabrowski09}.
This is the closest precedent for Voblint's context-indexed semantics. Their
analysis is specified by constraints that no solver computes. Voblint's
contexts are admitted by a relation, the totality condition makes the indexed
collection exhaustive, and the indexing is connected to an executable verified
solver. Trace partitioning indexes sets of traces by control
history~\cite{rival07}; Rival and Mauborgne allow coverings in which one trace
has several indices and present calling contexts as one such indexing. The
activation collecting semantics is a covering of this relational kind. Among
Isabelle developments, interprocedural analyses have been
verified~\cite{lammich07afp,wasserrab09afp}, but none of these entries claims
an extracted analyzer or represents an activation with its caller chain.

\subparagraph*{Verified solvers.}
Hofmann et al.\ verify a local generic solver in Coq~\cite{hofmann10}, and de
Vilhena et al.\ verify one that discovers dependencies dynamically in
Iris~\cite{devilhena20}. Stade et al.\ prove the top-down solver partially
correct in Isabelle/HOL~\cite{stade24}, and Tilscher et al.\ extend it to side
effects with a generic interface for update rules~\cite{tilscher26}. Voblint
vendors the latter and connects its certificate to programs. Termination is
proved for top-down solvers without side effects over a finite type of
unknowns~\cite{tilscher26jar}. Voblint's unknowns pair nodes with contexts. Each
program's graph is finite, but the node type is not, and entry-state contexts
need not be finitely many, so that result does not apply.

\subparagraph*{Goblint and local traces.}
Side-effecting constraint systems~\cite{seidl03,apinis12} and the split
between analyses and solvers are Goblint's architecture~\cite{vojdani16,seidl26},
and Voblint's \isaname{dg_spec} follows its \texttt{Spec} interface. The
correspondence is architectural; no theorem transfers to Goblint's OCaml
implementation. Schwarz et al.\ prove Goblint's thread-modular analyses sound
against local traces on paper~\cite{schwarz21,schwarz23}. Activation traces
adapt their construction to procedure activations. Activations do not
interfere, so no concurrency result of that work transfers.

\subparagraph*{Differences from prior work.}
Voblint inherits its solver, the side-effecting constraint systems, the
analysis interface, the shape of its traces, and the distinction between
call-string and functional contexts. What it adds is their mechanized
connection, from a small-step source semantics through a context-indexed
collecting semantics to a side-effecting equation system and its verified
solver, ending in a theorem about the exported analyzer. We know of no prior
mechanization of this connection. The
closest, Dabrowski and Pichardie~\cite{dabrowski09}, has contexts in the
semantics but no solver. Verasco~\cite{jourdan15} has an executable analyzer
for a far larger language. It reanalyzes a function at each call site,
raises an alarm at a possible recursive call, and computes its result by
structural iteration rather than with a generic solver.
