Calling Contexts Made Concrete: End-to-End
Soundness of a Goblint-Style Analyzer in
Isabelle/HOL
Manuel Lerchner #
Technical University of Munich, Germany

      Abstract
Goblint-style abstract interpreters translate a program into a side-effecting system of equations over
abstract states, one unknown per program point and calling context, and solve it with a generic
solver. The partial correctness of Goblint’s top-down solver has been verified, but that theorem does
not say whether the equations describe the program. Closing this gap requires a concrete meaning
for calling contexts, which the ordinary collecting semantics lacks because it merges the stores of
all calls to a procedure. We introduce activation traces, which follow one procedure activation
together with the activation that created it, and let a context policy relate each trace to the contexts
it may belong to. Soundness then reduces to five local obligations over an arbitrary control-flow
graph, and every post-solution of the generated equations meets them. In Isabelle/HOL, we prove
an end-to-end theorem from a small-step semantics of a small imperative language with recursive
procedures to the verdicts of the analyzer function, for every analysis configuration it offers. The
function is exported to OCaml and runs as a command-line tool. The result is partial correctness:
the solver’s termination is not proved.

 1      Introduction
A sound static analyzer over-approximates every execution of a program, so a property
that holds of the over-approximation holds in every run. The guarantee is only as good
as the analyzer’s implementation, and analyzers are large programs. Goblint, an abstract
interpreter for C, has reported no false negative on the SV-COMP benchmark suite since its
first participation in 2021 [24], yet its issue tracker lists more than a hundred issues labeled
as unsound in the same period. In one of them, the congruence domain claimed c % 2 ==
1 for c ∈ {−5, −7}, although C’s truncating remainder gives −1 for both values [20]. The
defect (issue 1156) was reported only after Goblint had claimed the false result.
    A machine-checked proof rules out such defects for every input, but only if a theorem
connects the proved parts to the tool the user runs. For Goblint’s architecture, one part has
been verified. Goblint translates a program into a side-effecting constraint system [23, 1],
whose right-hand sides may contribute to unknowns other than their own, and solves it with a

          Calling contexts make that missing link hard. A context-sensitive analysis keeps one
      unknown per program point and calling context. Suppose main calls bump(5) and bump(4),
      and bump returns its argument plus one. An analysis whose contexts record the argument
      claims the result 6 in one context and 5 in the other. The collecting semantics, the set of
      stores that executions bring to each program point, holds both results at the return of bump
      but forgets which call produced which store. A per-context claim therefore has nothing to
      be checked against. Among mechanized analyzers, Dabrowski and Pichardie instrument a
      concrete semantics with contexts, but their analysis is specified by constraints that no solver
      computes [6]. The other works we compare have no procedures, distinguish no contexts, or
      reanalyze a function at each call site (Section 8).
         We give calling contexts a concrete meaning and use it to verify a Goblint-style analyzer
      end to end. The development, called Voblint, is written in Isabelle/HOL and contains no
      sorry. Its contributions are:

         A concrete semantics of calling contexts. An activation trace records one procedure
         activation together with the activation that created it, and a context policy relates each
         trace to the contexts it may carry. The relation may admit several contexts for one call.
         Under a totality condition, splitting the collecting semantics by context loses no store
         (Section 3).

         A soundness contract of five local obligations. Any assignment of store sets to nodes and
         contexts that meets the obligations bounds every valid trace in every admitted context.
         The contract mentions no domain, equation, or solver (Section 3).

         A modular discharge of the contract. Every post-solution of the generated, context-routed
         equations meets the contract. Domains, context policies, and the solver each prove their
         own obligations, and the solver enters only through its certificate (Section 4).

         An end-to-end theorem about the shipped analyzer. Whenever the analyzer function
         returns a report, every store that a finite source execution from an initial store with
         zeroed globals reaches is covered by the report and satisfies its definite verdicts, for every
         configuration the function offers. The function is the one exported to OCaml (Section 5).
         Further theorems show that the result is not vacuous and that a domain obligation rejects
         the Goblint defect above (Section 7).
      The solver, side-effecting constraint systems, the local traces that activation traces adapt [21],
      and the architecture of separating analyses from solvers [28, 24] are prior work. Our
      contribution is their connection: to our knowledge, no prior mechanization leads from a
      source semantics through a context-indexed collecting semantics to a side-effecting constraint
      system and its verified solver.
         The guarantee has a precise scope. The source language, VIMP, has integer variables,
      global variables, and recursive procedures with parameters and return values, but no pointers,
      arrays, or heap. The result is partial correctness: the analyzer answers only when its solver
      run terminates, and termination is not proved for every program. The parser, Isabelle’s code
      generator, and the OCaml toolchain are trusted.

 2      Overview: One Call Through the Pipeline

We follow the program of Figure 1 through the analyzer under the interval domain with
entry-state contexts. The source program is compiled to a procedure-aware control-flow
graph (CFG) in which each procedure has an entry node FunctionEntry p and a result node
FunctionResult p, and each call is an edge from its call site to the callee’s entry, labeled with
its continuation in the caller. A proved forward simulation relates every source execution to
an execution of this graph with the same store (Section 5).
    From the graph, the analyzer generates equations over local unknowns (v, c) for a node v
and a context c, and global unknowns without a program point. At the call a = bump(5),
the right-hand side of the continuation reads the caller’s state, applies the analysis’s enter
operation, which binds n to [5, 5], and routes the resulting entry state to the callee context
c1 = [5, 5]. Which contexts a callee is entered in becomes known only while the equations
are solved, so the callee’s entry equation cannot list its callers. The call instead publishes its
entry state as a side effect to a global seed unknown for (bump, c1 ), which the entry equation
of bump in c1 reads. The continuation then reads the callee’s exit state in c1 and combines
it with the caller’s state. The second call routes to c2 = [4, 4], so the two activations use
separate copies of bump, and the analysis proves both checks.
    For these per-context claims to be sound, the concrete semantics has to say which stores
belong to c1 . Activation traces (Section 3) supply this. The trace of the activation bump(5)
records its caller, the policy admits it in c1 , and the activation collecting semantics at the
result of bump in c1 contains only the store with result 6. The soundness argument is then a
chain of inclusions at every node v:

       3      Activation Traces and the Coverage Contract
      A context-sensitive analysis claims, for each node v and context c, a set of stores. To check
      such a claim, we need the set of stores that executions bring to v in context c. Neither
      a graph execution nor its flat list of visited node–store pairs provides it. A frame of the
      runtime stack holds only what is needed to resume the caller and disappears at the return,
      and the flat list interleaves the steps of all activations. Grouping a run by activation makes
      the missing structure explicit: each call starts a new activation attached to its caller, and
      each return composes the finished callee back into that caller.


      3.1     Activation traces
      An activation path is a list of node–store pairs visited by one activation. An activation trace
      has one constructor for each way an activation begins or continues (activation_trace):

      datatype activation_trace =
         Root activation_path
       | Call (activation_trace_caller: activation_trace) activation_path
       | Resume (activation_trace_current: activation_trace) (activation_trace_callee: activation_trace)
         activation_path

      Root π is the activation of main. Call t′ π is a callee created by the caller trace t′ , frozen at
      the call site. Resume t′ t′′ π continues t′ past the call that produced the finished callee t′′ .
      Observers return the last node sink_node t, its store sink_store t, and the caller caller_of t,
      which a Root lacks.
          The datatype admits terms that no execution produces, for instance a Resume pairing a
      caller with a callee that another activation created. The set TG,g,S of valid traces of a graph
      g with initial stores S is therefore defined inductively, by four rules that mirror the rules of

destination (combine_collect). Because a callee stores its creator exactly, a return resumes

    Every finite source execution of a well-formed program yields a valid trace that ends
at the corresponding node with the same store and whose chain of callers represents the
runtime frame stack (source_run_has_activation_trace). Collecting the final stores of
valid traces per node gives the node collecting semantics CG,g,S (v) (node_collect).
    This design adapts the local traces of Schwarz et al. [21], which give one thread’s view of
a concurrent execution. Root corresponds to an initial trace, Call to thread creation, and
Resume to the binary rule for acquiring a lock, which incorporates a second trace. The objects
differ: a local trace is an acyclic graph of events of several threads, whereas an activation
trace holds the path of one activation, refers to at most two other traces, and its Resume
accepts only the callee that the activation itself created.



3.2    Calling contexts as a relation
An analysis chooses a callee’s context at the call. With call strings of length one, it is the
call site; with entry-state contexts, the abstract values of the formals at entry. A context
policy fixes this choice for every concrete call. It maps a call site u, a caller context c, a
description of the call, the caller’s store, and the entered store to the set of callee contexts it
admits (context_policy):

type-synonym ′c context_policy =
 cfg_node ⇒ ′c ⇒ call_info ⇒ store ⇒ store ⇒ ′c set


A set, because Goblint’s enter may return several alternatives for one call, each routed
separately. When the alternatives overlap, one concrete call belongs to several contexts
(ov_two_contexts_admitted). A set may also be empty, for instance at a call the analysis
considers unreachable.
    The contexts of a trace follow by applying the policy along the calls that created it
(activation_context_rel). The root carries the initial context c0 , a callee carries every
c′ that the policy admits for its call from some context of its caller, and local steps and
resumptions keep the caller’s contexts. The traces themselves store no context, so the policy
is a parameter of every theorem. The activation collecting semantics collects the final stores
of valid traces that end at v and carry c (activation_collect):

   AG,adm,c0 ,g,S (v, c) = { sink_store t | t ∈ TG,g,S , sink_node t = v, t carries c }.

In the running example, the final store of bump(5) lies in A(FunctionResult bump, [5, 5]) and
that of bump(4) in A(FunctionResult bump, [4, 4]), whereas C at that node contains both.

4.1    What an analysis supplies
An analysis describes the stores at a program point by an abstract state d and supplies the
record dg_spec, the counterpart of Goblint’s Spec interface: one transfer per kind of edge
action, an enter operation, and two combine operations. A call needs two states, because
after the return the caller’s locals come from before the call, while the globals and the result
come from the callee. Enter therefore returns a list of pairs of a resume state q, describing
the caller’s store, and an entry state e, describing the store the callee starts with. Combine
builds the state after the call from q and the callee’s exit state. The local soundness laws are
collected in the locale analysis_contract: the concretization γ is monotone, every transfer
loses no successor store, and combine covers combine(s, t) whenever q covers s and the exit
state covers t. Enter must return a pair covering the caller’s store and the entered store
(entry_pairs_cover).
    A numeric domain does not prove these laws itself. It implements abstract arithmetic,
comparisons, and guard refinement with their soundness laws, and the framework derives the
transfers and proves them sound once for all domains, following Nipkow and Klein’s generic
interpreter [18, Sect. 13.5]. The shipped domains are Sign, Interval, Parity, Congruence, and
their reduced product. None of these proofs mentions a context, a routing function, or a
solver.


4.2    Context-indexed equations with routing
For every node v and context c there is a local unknown (v, c). Its right-hand side joins four
sources: the initial state at the program entry, the transfer along each local edge into v in
the same context c, the combined result of each call returning to v, and, at a procedure entry,
the entry states published to it (routed_node_rhs_parts). At a call, a routing function
chooses the callee context from the call site, the caller context, and the entry state. Under
entry-state contexts the choice depends on a state that is known only during solving, so the
callee cannot list its callers in advance. Each call therefore publishes its entry state as a side
effect to a global seed unknown for the callee and context, which the callee’s entry equation
reads. A recursive call needs no special treatment: its equation reads an exit state that
depends on the caller’s own unknowns, and the solver handles the cycle as it handles a loop.
    The equations choose a callee context from an abstract state; the concrete semantics
chooses one from the concrete call through the policy adm. The two must agree. Adequacy

      post-solution on a set of unknowns that contains the program entry in context c0 and is

         The proof checks the five obligations for this cover, one source of the right-hand side each:
      the initial state gives Init, edge transfers give Intra, adequacy gives Call and Return,
      and totality gives Total. Theorem 1 then yields the inclusion. The theorem holds for every
      domain and every context policy that proves adequacy and totality.


      4.3    The solver enters through one certificate
      Theorem 2 needs a post-solution on a closed set of unknowns. The verified top-down
      solver [26] provides the certificate part_post_solution for the query x it was started on,
      its solved set V , and its result σ:

      abbreviation part_post_solution ::
        ( ′x, ′g, ′d::bounded_semilattice_sup_bot) eqsT ⇒ ′x ⇒ ( ′x + ′g ⇒ ′d) ⇒ ′x set ⇒ bool where
       part_post_solution T x sigma vars ≡
        x ∈ vars ∧ (∀ u ∈ vars.
        depL T sigma u ⊆ vars ∧
        eq T u sigma ≤ sigma (Inl u) ∧
        sides_of_rhs (T u) sigma ≤ sigma)

      The query is solved, the solved set is closed under the reads of right-hand sides, and σ bounds
      both the right-hand side and every side effect of each solved unknown. Two directions have
      to meet. The solver works backward from its query, the result of main, while Theorem 2
      follows executions forward from the entry. Every node of a procedure reaches its result node,
      because the compiler ends every body with a return edge, so closure under reads makes all
      nodes of a procedure solved in a context once its result is. live_unknowns_cover proves the
      forward closure from a well-formed program and a returned solve.
          The rest of the proof never inspects the solver. The analysis locale takes the solver as a
      parameter and assumes four facts about it (certified_solver): a run in its domain yields
      part_post_solution and a finite solved set, and a run of its executable form that returns lies
      in the domain and agrees with the logical solver. These facts are proved once for the vendored
      solver under each of its five update rules for global unknowns (td_certified_solver).
      Replacing the solver would not touch the semantic layer.

 5     The End-to-End Theorem
The previous sections bound the activation collecting semantics of a graph. Two links remain:
from source executions to graph executions, and from the solved valuation to the report the
user reads.

From source to graph. VIMP has a small-step semantics pstep over configurations of
a residual command, a store, and a frame stack. The compiler translates each procedure
body by continuations into a procedure-aware CFG, and a forward simulation matches every
source step by graph steps that reach the same store (csim_star). A source configuration
does not determine a graph node, since its residual command can occur at several places, as
in if (b) {x = 1;} else {x = 1;}. The run is therefore lifted to an elaborated program
whose annotated commands name their nodes, and the relation p, s0 ⊢ cfg ▷ v (at_node)
observes a configuration at the nodes of its lifted runs. It is a relation, and the theorem below
quantifies over every node at which a configuration is observed. Combined with Section 3,
every store a finite source execution reaches lies in C at a node the simulation relates to
the run (source_reaches_node_collect), and on the elaboration this holds at every node
where the run is observed (csim_point_compile_prog).

From valuation to report. The analyzer function run_voblint takes a configuration (the
analyses to run, the update rule for global unknowns, the context policy, including the
call-string length, and the placement of program globals) and a program. It rejects an invalid
configuration or an ill-formed program, and otherwise runs the executable solver and returns
Analysed res when the solve returns a result (run_voblint). The report lists, per node,
the solved states of every solved context, and classifies each check __voblint_check(b)
as PROVED, REFUTED, UNKNOWN, or DEAD. Rres (v) is the set of stores some state at v
describes, and Vres (v) the set of stores in which every PROVED check at v holds and every
REFUTED check fails.

       derives from the returned solve that every unknown an execution reaches was solved. It has
       no well-formedness premise: a malformed program does not yield Analysed. The statement
       holds for every analysis configuration the function accepts, because the configuration is a
       quantified argument.


       What verdicts mean. PROVED states that whenever a covered execution reaches the check,
       the condition holds; REFUTED that it fails (run_voblint_check_sound). Neither asserts that
       the check is reachable, and REFUTED is not a verified counterexample. DEAD is the reachability
       claim: nothing is collected at the check’s node (run_voblint_dead_check_unreached). At a
       node without an arithmetic diagnostic, no divisor on an edge leaving it is zero in any collected
       store (run_voblint_arithmetic_safe). An error means that the divisor is zero wherever a
       covered execution reaches the node (run_voblint_arithmetic_refuted); a warning means
       only that the analysis could not exclude zero.


       What is trusted. Figure 3 separates what is proved from what is trusted. The theorem
       is about the constant that Isabelle’s code generator [9] exports to OCaml and that the
       command-line tool calls. The lexer and parser, which construct the program, and the
       handwritten OCaml that prints the report lie outside the proof. The Isabelle kernel, the code
       generator, the OCaml toolchain, and the arbitrary-precision integer library are trusted. That
       pstep models the intended language is argued, not proved: VIMP integers are unbounded,
       division by zero is defined, and a callee’s locals start at zero, so a verdict need not hold for a
       C program with the same text.


        6      Mechanization

       The development [14] comprises 212 Isabelle/HOL theories with 59,107 lines, of which 39,236
       are formal text, and 2,683 proved statements. It reuses 7 theories (9,533 lines) of the verified
       solver development [26], vendored with four changes proposed upstream (a build fix, renames,
       a removed assumption, and added facts) that leave its algorithm unchanged. Neither Voblint
       nor the reused solver theories contain sorry or oops. Code generation emits 14,095 lines
       of OCaml, and 4,919 lines of handwritten OCaml parse programs and print reports. A full
       build of the development takes several minutes on an Apple M4 Pro laptop.

Design choices and their alternatives. Four choices shaped the proof; the first replaced a
prototype.
   Activation traces as the soundness target. A first prototype used whole-program executions
   with calls inlined. To account for one call, the proof built a separate execution from the
   callee’s entry and spliced it into the caller’s run. This handled single calls but did not
   reflect the call stack and was hard to extend to recursion. Activation traces replaced it:
   a return resumes exactly the suspended caller, so per-context return flow follows from
   the trace structure.
   Contexts as a relation. The unit and call-string policies are functions and discharge
   Total once for every cover. The relational form is needed for an enter that returns
   overlapping alternatives and for the entry-state policy, which admits no context for stores
   the analysis excludes and proves Total from the solution.
   The solver as a parameter. The analysis locale assumes four facts about a solver rather
   than reasoning about the top-down solver’s run. The vendored solver discharges them
   under all five update rules, and the semantic proofs never unfold its definition.
   Two-default executable states. Soundness is stated over pointwise states, total functions
   on infinitely many variable names. The solver runs on a quotient type [11] of a default
   and a finite override list, as in Concrete Semantics [18, §13.6], but with two defaults:
   ⊤ for locals and 0♯ for globals, because VIMP zero-initializes globals. Each operation
   carries a theorem relating it to its pointwise counterpart (exec_local_spec_sound).


Isabelle mechanisms. Locales [2] carry the reusable obligations: the coverage contract, the
analysis contract, the routing conditions, and the certified solver are each a locale, and the
generic theorems are proved in their contexts. Type classes carry the lattice structure of
abstract values. Each domain registers with one locale interpretation per context policy.


Feedback on the proof assistant. Three points cost effort that the tooling could reduce.
First, HOL admits only terminating recursion, so the solver is defined with a domain predicate
and an executable variant that returns only when the recursion finishes; a returned run
must then be transported to the logical definition, which a lemma added to the vendored
solver proves once (solve_dom_of_solve_c). Second, code generation turns finite sets and
maps into lists that every lookup scans. One definition recompiled the whole program at
every equation evaluation until a proved code equation replaced it, without changing any
theorem; the remaining list-based structures dominate the running time (Section 7), and the
Collections Framework or the Containers library could replace them in the code setup alone.
Third, the kernel checks that a proof is valid, not that it is readable or reusable. Unused,
duplicated, and subsumed lemmas accumulated, so we built a separate linter for Isar theories
whose checks complete in seconds, against several minutes for a full build; agents that edit
theories can run it after individual changes, and the full build establishes correctness.


Use of generative AI. Once the definitions and interfaces had stabilized, generative AI
tools produced many of the proofs. Isabelle checks their validity relative to the definitions;
human review concentrated on the definitions, the assumptions, and the meaning of the
theorems, which no kernel checks. The declaration at the end of the paper states the extent
of this use.

        7     Evaluation
       A machine-checked theorem can hold for uninteresting reasons. Its premises may never
       hold together, its obligations may exclude no error a real analyzer makes, and an analyzer
       that answers UNKNOWN everywhere satisfies it as well (unknown_everywhere_sound). We
       therefore check that the premises can hold and that the obligations have force, and then
       measure precision on concrete programs. Facts about one fixed program are proved by eval,
       which runs generated code and therefore also trusts the code generator.

       The theorem is not vacuous (machine-checked). For the program of Figure 1, analyzed
       with Interval, entry-state contexts, and the warrowing update rule, a theory builds a source
       execution that returns from both calls, evaluation shows that the analyzer answers with
       both checks PROVED, and nv_source_certified instantiates Theorem 3 with every premise
       discharged. Companion instances do the same for single checks (nv_check_proved_sound)
       and for a DEAD check (nv_dead_unreached).

       The obligations reject a real defect (machine-checked). In the Goblint defect of Section 1,
       the congruence domain returned the constant 1 for (1 + 2Z) mod 2 [20]. In Voblint, every
       congruence remainder must satisfy congruence_mod_sound, which requires the truncating
       remainder of any two concrete values to lie in the concretization of the abstract result. The
       pre-fix answer violates this at −5, and prefix_congruence_mod_unsound proves that every
       abstract remainder returning the constant does. The defective operator could not have been
       registered as a domain. Total has force as well: total_dropped_unsound exhibits a cover
       that meets the other four obligations and misses a reached store.

       Contexts buy precision (machine-checked for one program). When f passes its argu-
       ment to g and main calls f with a positive and a negative value, call strings of length
       one merge both activations of g and Sign loses both signs, while length two keeps them
       apart and proves both checks. The theories prove the separation at the entry of g
       (sign_k2_strictly_more_precise_than_k1_at_g). This is a witness for one program,
       not a theorem that longer call strings dominate.

Running time (empirical). Speed was not a goal: the generated code runs as Isabelle
emits it, with one proved code equation added to remove a recompilation of the program
at every equation evaluation. Over the 369 regression fixtures under 15 combinations of
domain and context policy, three repetitions each on an Apple M4 Pro, the median solve
takes 0.23 ms and the 95th percentile 1.48 ms; 21 of 5,535 runs exceed the 30 s limit. The
expensive cases include recursive programs under entry-state contexts, where domains such
as Interval create one context per recursion level. On generated programs that grow one
size at a time, the number of solver evaluations grows about linearly, while solve time grows
with exponents between 2.01 and 2.86, because the generated code represents finite sets
and maps as lists. These measurements cover small programs on one machine and imply no
performance bound.


 8     Related Work

Verified analyzers. Verasco is a Coq-verified analyzer for most of C99, excluding recursion
and dynamic allocation [12]. Its interpreter iterates over the structure of C#minor, reanalyzes
a function at every call site, and raises an alarm at a possible recursive call. It covers a far
larger language than Voblint, so the comparison concerns design: Voblint turns calls into
equations over unknowns indexed by node and context and computes them with a generic
solver. The value analysis of Blazy et al. checks the candidate of an untrusted iterator with a
verified checker [3]; Voblint instead runs a solver whose partial correctness is proved. Nipkow
and Klein develop abstract interpretation for IMP in Isabelle/HOL [17, 18], and Voblint’s
non-relational domains follow their generic interpreter. Their language has no procedures.
Franceschino et al. verify an IMP analyzer in F* that runs in a browser [8], and Cachera and
Pichardie extract a certified denotational interpreter [5]; neither has procedures. Michelland
et al. make soundness proofs reusable by sharing one interpreter between the concrete and
the abstract semantics [15]. Cachera et al. compute a context-insensitive dataflow analysis
with a verified solver for ordinary inequations [4].

       Contexts in mechanized semantics. Dabrowski and Pichardie instrument a concrete
       semantics with contexts through a module type whose call-context function computes a
       callee’s context, and prove a context-sensitive points-to analysis sound in Coq [6]. This is
       the closest precedent for Voblint’s context-indexed semantics. Their analysis is specified
       by constraints that no solver computes. Voblint’s contexts are admitted by a relation, the
       totality condition makes the indexed collection exhaustive, and the indexing is connected to
       an executable verified solver. Trace partitioning indexes sets of traces by control history [19];
       Rival and Mauborgne allow coverings in which one trace has several indices and present
       calling contexts as one such indexing. The activation collecting semantics is a covering
       of this relational kind. Among Isabelle developments, interprocedural analyses have been
       verified [13, 29], but none of these entries claims an extracted analyzer or represents an
       activation with its caller chain.

       Verified solvers. Hofmann et al. verify a local generic solver in Coq [10], and de Vilhena
       et al. verify one that discovers dependencies dynamically in Iris [7]. Stade et al. prove the
       top-down solver partially correct in Isabelle/HOL [25], and Tilscher et al. extend it to side
       effects with a generic interface for update rules [26]. Voblint vendors the latter and connects
       its certificate to programs. Termination is proved for top-down solvers without side effects
       over a finite type of unknowns [27]; Voblint’s unknowns pair nodes, of an infinite type, with
       contexts, so that result does not apply.

       Goblint and local traces. Side-effecting constraint systems [23, 1] and the split between
       analyses and solvers are Goblint’s architecture [28, 24], and Voblint’s dg_spec follows its
       Spec interface. The correspondence is architectural; no theorem transfers to Goblint’s OCaml
       implementation. Schwarz et al. prove Goblint’s thread-modular analyses sound against local
       traces on paper [21, 22]. Activation traces adapt their construction to procedure activations.
       Activations do not interfere, so no concurrency result of that work transfers.


        9     Conclusion
       Activation traces give calling contexts a concrete counterpart, and with it the soundness of a
       Goblint-style analyzer reduces to five local obligations that a solved, context-routed equation
       system meets. The resulting theorem covers the analyzer function that is exported and run,
       for every configuration it offers.
           The result has limits we have stated where they apply and collect here. It is partial
       correctness, since the solver’s termination is a per-program fact established by running it.
       VIMP is scalar: it has no pointers, arrays, heap, or threads, and the adequacy of its semantics
       is argued, not proved. No precision or completeness property is proved.
           Several results do not depend on the details of VIMP: the solver certificate is stated over
       unknowns and right-hand sides, and the coverage contract refers to the language only through
       a few step functions. Features that change only these step functions, such as machine integers,
       leave the contract and its composition intact. The trace semantics, however, assumes that a
       callee cannot modify its caller’s locals, so a return can resume the caller with the locals it
       had before the call. Pointers into the caller’s frame, a shared heap, or threads break this
       assumption and would require extending the trace semantics itself.
