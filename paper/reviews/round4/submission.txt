End-to-End Soundness of a Context-Sensitive
Goblint-Style Analyzer in Isabelle/HOL
Manuel Lerchner #
Technical University of Munich, Germany

      Abstract
Goblint-style abstract interpreters translate a program into side-effecting equations, with one
unknown per program point and calling context, and solve them with a generic solver. The partial
correctness of that solver has been verified in Isabelle/HOL, but the theorem does not connect the
equations to the program. We mechanize this connection for a small imperative language with
recursive procedures. We adapt the local traces of Schwarz et al. to procedure activations, because
a return must resume exactly the activation that made the call. A context policy relates each
activation trace to the calling contexts it may belong to. The development provides (i) a coverage
contract of five local obligations over an arbitrary control-flow graph, which bounds the final store
of every valid trace in every admitted context; (ii) a proof that every post-solution of the generated,
context-routed equations (a valuation that satisfies all of them) meets the contract when the context
policy agrees with the routing, using the verified solver only through its certificate; and (iii) an
end-to-end theorem from a small-step source semantics to the verdicts of the analyzer function that
Isabelle exports to OCaml, for every configuration it offers. The guarantee is per program point:
every reached store is covered by the report and satisfies its definite verdicts. It is partial correctness,
since solver termination is not proved, and the language has no pointers or heap.

 1      Introduction

A context-sensitive static analyzer keeps a separate abstract state for each program point
and calling context. In the program of Figure 1, main calls bump(5) and bump(4), and bump
returns its argument plus one. An interval analysis with one state per program point must
describe both calls at the return of bump. It therefore cannot prove either check. With
contexts that record the argument, it analyzes bump once per argument and proves both.
Goblint, an abstract interpreter for C [36, 31], gains this precision by translating a program
into a side-effecting constraint system [30, 1]. This is a system of inequations over unknowns
indexed by program point and context, whose right-hand sides may also contribute to other
unknowns. A generic top-down solver then solves the system. Goblint’s soundness rests on
tests: its issue tracker lists more than a hundred issues labeled as unsound since 2021, and a
defect in any of these layers can turn a verdict false.

          That solver has been verified. Stade et al. proved it partially correct in Isabelle/HOL [33],
      and Tilscher et al. extended the proof to side effects [34]. When the solver terminates, it
      returns a post-solution, a valuation that satisfies the inequations, on the unknowns it solved,
      a set closed under the reads of right-hand sides. Whether the equations describe the program
      lies outside that theorem. Closing the gap needs a concrete semantics in which a per-context
      state has something to cover. Such semantics are classical on paper [32, 16, 14, 12] (Section 8).
      What has not been mechanized, to our knowledge, is the connection from a source semantics
      through such a context-indexed semantics to a side-effecting constraint system and its verified
      solver.
          We mechanize this connection. The development, Voblint, is written in Isabelle/HOL
      and contains no sorry. Its parts are the following.
          We adapt the local traces of Schwarz et al. [28] to procedure activations. A context policy,
          which may admit several contexts for one call, relates these activation traces to contexts.
          We prove that five local obligations on a cover bound every valid trace in every admitted
          context, over an arbitrary control-flow graph (Section 3).
          We prove that every post-solution of the generated, context-routed equations meets these
          obligations, provided the context policy agrees with the routing of the equations. The
          verified solver enters only through its certificate (Section 4).
          We prove an end-to-end theorem about the analyzer function that Isabelle exports to
          OCaml. Whenever it returns a report, every store a finite source execution reaches is
          covered by the report at its program point and satisfies the definite verdicts there, for
          every configuration (Section 5).
          We show that the theorem is not vacuous, that a domain obligation rejects a real Goblint
          defect, and how calling contexts change verdicts on recursive programs (Section 7).
      The solver, side-effecting constraint systems, local traces, and Goblint’s split between analyses
      and solvers are prior work. The context-indexed semantics is the structure of our proof. The
      shipped guarantee is per program point. It is not a separate certificate for each context’s
      row of the report (Section 5).
          The scope is limited. The source language, VIMP, has integer variables, global variables,
      and recursive procedures with parameters and return values, but no pointers, arrays, or
      heap. The result is partial correctness: the analyzer answers only when its solver run
      terminates, and termination is not proved for every program. The parser and the report
      printer are unverified handwritten code. Isabelle’s code generator, the OCaml toolchain, and
      a solver-tracing hook are trusted.

      execution to a graph execution with the same store (Section 5).

    For these per-context states to mean something, the concrete semantics has to say which
stores belong to c1 . Activation traces (Section 3) supply this. The trace of the activation
bump(5) records its caller, the context policy admits it in c1 , and the activation collecting

collecting semantics C(v) collects them regardless of context. A report res of the analyzer
defines two sets at each node v. The report set Rres (v) contains the stores that some state
of the report at v describes, in any context. The verdict set Vres (v) contains the stores in
which every definite verdict at v holds. The soundness argument is a chain of inclusions at
every node:
                                 S

Each inclusion may add unreached stores, which costs precision, but none may lose a reached
store. Contexts appear only in the middle of the chain; the end-to-end theorem (Section 5)
states its outer inclusions.

 3      Activation Traces and the Coverage Contract
This section has three ingredients. Valid activation traces are adapted from the local traces
of Schwarz et al. [28]. Context policies read a calling context off the concrete call, as the
call-string approach of Sharir and Pnueli tags information with call history [32]. Their
relational form is ours. The coverage contract and its soundness theorem are ours. Nothing
here mentions an abstract domain, an equation, or a solver.
    A context-sensitive analysis claims, for each node v and context c, a set of stores. Checking



                                                                                                               ITP 2027

      such a claim needs the stores that executions bring to v in context c. A graph execution with
      a plain frame stack does not record contexts: a frame holds only what is needed to resume
      the caller and disappears at the return. Frames could carry a ghost context (Section 6).
      We instead keep contexts out of the semantics. Grouping a run by activation makes the
      missing structure explicit: each call starts an activation attached to its caller, and each
      return composes the finished callee back into that caller.

      3.1    Activation traces
      An activation path is the list of node–store pairs visited by one activation. An activation trace
      has one constructor for each way an activation begins or continues (activation_trace):
      Root π is the activation of main, Call t′ π is a callee whose creator t′ is frozen at the call
      site, and Resume t′ t′′ π continues t′ past the call that produced the finished callee t′′ . The
      observers path_of t, sink_node t and sink_store t return the path and its last node and
      store, caller_of t returns Some of the creating caller, and extend t (v, s) appends a step to
      the path.
          The datatype admits terms that no execution produces. The set TG,g,S of valid traces of
      a graph g with initial stores S, where entry g is the entry node of main, is defined by four
      rules that mirror the rules of graph execution (valid_activation_trace):

      thread creation, and Resume to the binary rule for acquiring a lock, which incorporates a
      second trace (Section 8).

      3.2    Calling contexts as a relation
      An analysis chooses a callee’s context at the call: with call strings of length k, the last k
      call sites; with entry-state contexts, the abstract values of the formals at entry. A context
M. Lerchner                                                                                              1:5


policy fixes this choice for every concrete call. It maps a call site u, a caller context c, a
description of the call, the caller’s store, and the entered store to the set of admitted callee
contexts (context_policy). The result is a set because Goblint’s enter may return several
alternatives for one call, each routed separately. The set may be empty, for instance at a call
the analysis considers unreachable. The shipped analyses’ enter returns a single alternative,
so a call is admitted in at most one context per caller context. The relation matters for an
enter with several alternatives: in a regression theory, a Sign analysis whose enter returns
two overlapping alternatives (the plain entry state, and one that forgets the formals) admits
one concrete call in two contexts (ov_two_contexts_admitted).
    For a call edge e into p, we write adm u,c (e, s, s′ ) for the set the policy returns for the
call described by e and p, caller store s and entered store s′ . We write c′ ∈ adm ∃u,c (p, s, s′ )
when some call edge from u into p has entered store s′ from s and the policy admits c′
for it (admits_call_context). The contexts a trace carries are then defined inductively
(activation_context_rel):

                                  t′ carries c
    Root π carries c0        Resume t′ t′′ π carries c
    t carries c c′ ∈ adm ∃sink_node t, c (p, sink_store t, s′ )
         Call t ((FunctionEntry p, s′ ) # π) carries c′
That is, the root carries the initial context c0 , a callee carries every context admitted for
its call from some context of its caller, and a resumed activation keeps the contexts it had
before the call. Traces store no context, so the policy is a parameter of every theorem below.
The activation collecting semantics collects the final stores of the valid traces that end at v
and carry c (activation_collect):

   AG,adm,c0 ,g,S (v, c) = { sink_store t | t ∈ TG,g,S , sink_node t = v, t carries c }.

In the running example, the final store of bump(5) lies in A(FunctionResult bump, [5, 5]) and
that of bump(4) in A(FunctionResult bump, [4, 4]), whereas C at that node contains both.

3.3    The coverage contract
A cover assigns a set of stores to each node and context. Valid traces grow by four rules, so
it suffices to check a cover against each rule once. The contract is the locale activation_
coverage, Isabelle’s mechanism for a named list of parameters and assumptions. It has one
obligation per rule plus a totality condition. For every call edge (u, e, FunctionEntry p, k):

   Init            s ∈ S =⇒ s ∈ cover(entry g , c0 )
   Intra           (u, a, v) ∈ intra g, s ∈ cover(u, c), s′ ∈ stepa (s) =⇒ s′ ∈ cover(v, c)
   Call            s ∈ cover(u, c), c′ ∈ adm u,c (e, s, enter e (s))
                        =⇒ enter e (s) ∈ cover(FunctionEntry p, c′ )
   Return          s ∈ cover(u, c), c′ ∈ adm ∃u,c (p′ , s, s′ ),
                      t ∈ cover(FunctionResult p, c′ ) =⇒ combine e (s, t) ∈ cover(k, c)
   Total           s ∈ cover(u, c) =⇒ adm u,c (e, s, enter e (s)) ̸= ∅

Return is quantified over every procedure p′ and entered store s′ admitted at the call site,
not only over the edge e it returns through. This matches the return rule, which does not tie
the returning edge to the entering one. On a graph with one call edge per call site, p′ = p

      four leave. If a call from a covered store is admitted in no context, Call and Return
      hold vacuously and nothing places the store after the return at the continuation, although

      other four obligations can miss a reached store (total_dropped_unsound). Total asks only

      some context and that its final store is covered in every context it carries. A single-trace
      invariant does not suffice for the return rule, which needs the invariant for the caller recovered

      of callers (caller_chain_covered, an instance of the induction principle caller_chain_

      return case, for each context c of the resumed caller, it re-admits the callee at some c′ from
      c along the callee’s own entry edge. The callee then carries c′ (activation_context_rel_

      is why Return quantifies over the admitted entry (p′ , s′ ) rather than over the returning
      edge. Within the contract, Total also gives every valid trace a context, so splitting by
      context loses nothing (node_collect_eq_Union_activation_collect):

       4     From Equations to a Cover
      The contract of Section 3 asks for a cover. This section constructs one from a solved equation
      system. The ingredients have different origins. The analysis interface mirrors Goblint’s
      Spec [36, 31]. Side-effecting equations come from Seidl et al. and Apinis et al. [30, 1]. The
      solver and its certificate are those of Tilscher et al. [34]. The routing conditions, which
      connect the equations to the context policy, and their discharge for each policy are ours.

      4.1    What an analysis supplies
      An analysis describes the stores at a node by an abstract state d. Its meaning γ(d, ρ) also
      depends on ρ, the solved values of the analysis’s global unknowns. We suppress ρ when it
      plays no role. The analysis supplies the record dg_spec, the counterpart of Goblint’s Spec:
      one transfer per kind of edge action, an enter operation, and two combine operations. A
      call needs two states. After the return, the caller’s locals come from before the call, while
      the globals and the result come from the callee. Enter therefore returns a list of entry pairs
      (q, d): a resume state q that describes the caller’s store, and an entry state d that describes
      the store in which the callee starts. After the return, combine_env merges the callee’s exit
      state into q, taking the callee’s globals, and combine_assign then writes the return value to
      the call’s destination. Together they mirror the concrete combine e of Section 3. A transfer
M. Lerchner                                                                                              1:7


may also publish to the analysis’s global unknowns, and its output is read in the previous
global values joined with what it publishes.
   The local soundness laws of transfers and combine form the locale analysis_contract:
the concretization is monotone, every transfer loses no successor store, and combine covers
combine e (s, t) whenever q covers s and the exit state covers t. Enter is not part of that
locale. That enter returns a pair covering the caller’s store and the entered store (entry_
pairs_cover) is used by the routing conditions below, which each context policy discharges.
A numeric domain proves only its abstract operations sound. The framework derives the
transfers once for all domains, following Nipkow and Klein’s generic interpreter [24, Sect. 13.5].
The shipped domains are Sign, Interval, Parity, Congruence, and their reduced product Int.

4.2    Context-indexed equations and routing
For every node v and context c there is a local unknown (v, c). Its right-hand side joins four
sources (routed_node_rhs_parts):
   the initial state at the program entry;
   the transfer along each intraprocedural edge into v, in the same context c;
   the combined result of each call returning to v;
   at a procedure entry, the entry states published to it.
At a call, a routing function route chooses the callee context from the call site, the caller
context, the entry state and the call. For a call edge (u, e, FunctionEntry p, k) and caller
context c, the right-hand side of the continuation (k, c) contains, for every entry pair (q, d)
that enter returns for σ(u, c) (routed_call_alternative_program): combine(q, ⊥) if d is
bottom, and otherwise, with c′ = route(u, c, d, e), the program

   publish d to seed(p, c′ );    r ← read (FunctionResult p, c′ );    combine(q, r).

The entry equation of (FunctionEntry p, c′ ) reads the global unknown seed(p, c′ ). In the
running example, the call bump(5) in c0 publishes n ∈ [5, 5] to seed(bump, [5, 5]), reads the
result of bump in [5, 5], and combines it with the resume state of main. A recursive call needs
no special treatment: its equation reads a result that depends on the caller’s own unknowns,
and the solver handles the cycle as it handles a loop.
    The equations choose a callee context from an abstract state, while the concrete semantics
chooses one from the concrete call through the policy adm. Two conditions make the choices
agree. Both are assumptions of the locale routed_context.
Adequacy (routing_adequate). If adm admits c′ for a call from a store that the caller’s
    solved state describes, then enter produces a pair (q, d) that is routed to c′ , whose resume
    state covers the caller’s store and whose entry state covers the entered store. The callee’s
    entry unknown in c′ is solved.
Totality (routing_total). adm admits at least one context for every call from a store that
    the caller’s solved state describes.
For the unit policy and for call strings, adm admits exactly the context the routing function
computes from the call site and the caller context. Totality is then immediate, and adequacy
reduces to entry coverage and the routed entry unknown being solved.
    For entry-state contexts, the policy routed_entry_context_rel is defined from the
solved valuation σ:

   adm u,c (e, s, s′ ) = { route(u, c, d, e) |
                                                 (q, d) ∈ enter e (σ(u, c)), s ∈ γ(q), s′ ∈ γ(d) }.

      4.3    The solver enters through one certificate
      Theorem 2 needs a set W of unknowns that contains the program entry, is closed under
      execution steps, and on which σ is bounded. The verified top-down solver [34] provides a
      different certificate, part_post_solution, for the query x it started on, its solved set V ,
      and its result σ: the query is in V , V is closed under the reads of right-hand sides, and σ
      bounds both the right-hand side and every side effect of each solved unknown. The two
      certificates point in different directions. The solver works backward from its query, the result
M. Lerchner                                                                                         1:9


of main, while Theorem 2 follows executions forward from the entry. The bridge takes W to
be the live part of V : the unknowns (u, c) such that u belongs to a procedure q whose result
(FunctionResult q, c) is in V (live_unknowns). The compiler ends every body with a return
edge, so every node of a procedure reaches the procedure’s result through intraprocedural
edges and call-to-continuation steps. The result’s right-hand side reads its predecessors,
so closure under reads puts all nodes of a procedure into V once its result is in V in that
context. Calls carry this into callees. A continuation (k, c) in V reads the callee’s result
(FunctionResult p, c′ ) whenever the routed entry state is not bottom (sol_vars_callee_
result), so the callee’s nodes are live in c′ , its entry among them. For a bottom entry
state, no store enters the callee and nothing is needed. live_unknowns_cover proves, from
a well-formed program and a returned solve, that the live set contains the program entry and
satisfies the closure hypotheses of Table 1. This is structural reachability in the graph. It
holds also for an activation that never returns, because the argument follows the equations,
not executions. Three sets are involved: the solver’s solved set V , its live subset W , on
which Theorem 2 is applied, and the states the report publishes, which are taken from V .
Publishing more states than the proof uses is sound, because the report only has to contain
the cover.
    The rest of the proof never inspects the solver. The analysis locale takes the solver as a
parameter and assumes four facts about it (certified_solver): a run in its domain yields
part_post_solution and a finite solved set, and a run of its executable form that returns
lies in the domain and agrees with the logical solver. These facts are proved for the vendored
solver under each of its five update rules for global unknowns (td_certified_solver). An
update rule decides how contributions to a global unknown accumulate: by join, by join per
contributing right-hand side, or by warrowing, which combines widening and narrowing [34].
Replacing the solver would not touch the semantic layer.


 5     The End-to-End Theorem

The previous sections bound the activation collecting semantics of a graph. Two links remain:
from source executions to graph executions, and from the solved valuation to the report the
user reads. The forward simulation follows the CompCert methodology [19]. The elaboration
that locates a source configuration at graph nodes, and the composition, are ours.


From source to graph. VIMP has a small-step semantics pstep over configurations (r, s, frs)
of a residual command, a store, and a frame stack. A call pushes a frame holding the caller’s
store and destination and continues with the callee’s body followed by a Restore marker;
the marker pops the frame and combines the stores as in Section 3 (pstep, rules Call and
RestoreStep). A call whose arity does not match, or whose callee is undeclared, is stuck;
well-formedness excludes such programs. The compiler translates each procedure body by
continuations into a procedure-aware CFG, and a forward simulation matches every source
step by graph steps that reach the same store (csim_star). A source configuration does not
determine a graph node, since its residual command can occur at several places, as in if (b)
{x = 1;} else {x = 1;}. The program is therefore elaborated into one whose commands
are annotated with their nodes. The relation p, s0 ⊢ (r, s, frs) ▷ v (at_node) holds when some
run of the elaborated program from s0 reaches an annotated configuration that erases to
(r, s, frs), names v, and is related to v by the simulation. In the example, the configuration
after either branch is observed at both branch nodes. It is a relation, and the theorem below
quantifies over every node at which a configuration is observed. Combined with Section 3,

       From valuation to report. The analyzer function run_voblint takes a configuration (the
       analyses to run, the update rule for global unknowns, the context policy, including the
       call-string length, and the placement of program globals) and a program. It rejects an invalid
       configuration or an ill-formed program, and otherwise runs the executable solver and returns
       Analysed res when the solve returns a result (run_voblint). The report lists, per node,
       the solved states of every solved context, and classifies each check __voblint_check(b)
       as PROVED, REFUTED, UNKNOWN, or DEAD. As in Section 2, Rres (v) is the set of stores
       that some state at v describes, in any context, and Vres (v) the set of stores in which every
       PROVED check at v holds and every REFUTED check fails.

       storage duration and locals are unconstrained; a finite source execution from the body of
       main with any stopping point, so nonterminating programs are covered through their prefixes;
       and a returned report. The conclusion has two halves: the reached configuration is observed
       at some node, and at every node where it is observed, its store is collected, covered by the
       report, and consistent with every definite verdict there. The contexts appear only inside the

       proof, through C(v) = c A(v, c) and Theorem 2. The guarantee is therefore per program

       state as the row for c. Closing this gap is future work (Section 9).

What verdicts mean. PROVED states that whenever a covered execution reaches the check,
the condition holds; REFUTED that it fails (run_voblint_check_sound). Neither asserts
that the check is reachable, and REFUTED is not a verified counterexample. DEAD is the
reachability claim: nothing is collected at the check’s node (run_voblint_dead_check_
unreached). Analogous theorems cover arithmetic diagnostics: without a diagnostic at a
node, no divisor leaving it is zero (run_voblint_arithmetic_safe), and an error means the
divisor is zero there (run_voblint_arithmetic_refuted).


What is trusted. Figure 2 separates what is proved from what is trusted. The theorem
is about the constant that Isabelle’s code generator [10] exports to OCaml and that the
command-line tool calls. The lexer and parser, which construct the program, and the
handwritten OCaml that prints the report lie outside the proof. The Isabelle kernel, the code
generator, the OCaml toolchain, and the arbitrary-precision integer library are trusted. So is
one handwritten OCaml function. For tracing, the exported code maps the logical constant
trace_event, which is the unit, to a handwritten hook (Solver_trace_hook.emit) that
the solve, the routing functions and the report construction call. The proofs treat each call
as the unit, and the trusted base assumes that the hook does not change the results.
    That pstep models the intended language is argued, not proved. Some of VIMP’s choices
differ from C. Integers are unbounded. Division by zero yields 0 and the remainder by zero
yields the dividend. Expressions are pure and total, so && and || have no evaluation order
to model. A failed check does not stop the execution, so a verdict for one check does not
depend on an earlier one. The locals of main start unconstrained, while a callee’s locals start
at 0. min, max, and the nondeterministic input are built-in calls, and main has no explicit
return. A verdict therefore need not hold for a C program with the same text.

       Design choices and their alternatives. Besides the relational context policy (Section 3)
       and the solver as a parameter (Section 4), two choices shaped the proof. Only for the first
       was an alternative implemented and abandoned.
          Activation traces as the soundness target. A first prototype used whole-program executions
          with calls inlined. To account for one call, the proof built a separate execution from the
          callee’s entry and spliced it into the caller’s run. This handled single calls but did not
          reflect the call stack and was hard to extend to recursion. The obvious alternative, which
          we did not implement, is the graph semantics itself, with a frame stack whose frames
          carry a ghost context. Its soundness proof would carry an invariant over the whole stack.
          Activation traces turn it into a fact about the caller chain, recovered through caller_of ,
          and keep contexts out of the concrete semantics, so the policy can be a parameter.
          Two-default executable states. Soundness is stated over total functions on variable names;
          the solver runs on a quotient type [13] of a finite override list with separate defaults for
          locals and zero-initialized globals (exec_local_spec_sound), refining the single default
          of Concrete Semantics [24, §13.6].

           The contracts of Sections 3 and 4 and the certified solver are locales [2]; a domain is
       registered with one locale interpretation per context policy.


       Feedback on the proof assistant. Two points cost effort that the tooling could reduce.
       First, functions in Isabelle/HOL are total. A solver that may diverge is therefore defined
       with a domain predicate, next to an executable variant that returns only when the recursion
       finishes, and a returned run must be transported to the logical definition. A lemma added
       to the vendored solver proves this transport once (solve_dom_of_solve_c). Second, code
       generation turns finite sets and maps into lists that every lookup scans, and CPU profiles
       attribute most of the solve time to these scans. The Containers library could replace them
       in the code setup alone, without touching the proofs; we leave this to future work.

 7     Evaluation
A machine-checked theorem can hold for uninteresting reasons. Its premises may never
hold together, its obligations may exclude no error a real analyzer makes, and an analyzer
that answers UNKNOWN everywhere satisfies it as well (unknown_everywhere_sound). We
therefore check that the premises can hold and that the obligations have force, and then
measure what contexts and domains buy on concrete programs. Each item states its kind of
evidence. Facts about one fixed program are proved by eval, which runs generated code
and therefore also trusts the code generator. Analyzer outputs are registered claims that
pixi run thesis-claims re-checks; Tables 3 and 4 are generated from claims and from the
committed profiling data.

The theorem is not vacuous (machine-checked, one program each). For the program
of Figure 1, analyzed with Interval, entry-state contexts, and the warrowing update rule,
a theory builds a source execution that returns from both calls. Evaluation shows that
the analyzer answers with both checks PROVED, and nv_source_certified instantiates
Theorem 3 with every premise discharged. Companion instances do the same for a single
check (nv_check_proved_sound) and for a DEAD check (nv_dead_unreached). They show
that the premises are satisfiable.

The obligations have force (machine-checked). Total is needed: total_dropped_
unsound exhibits a cover that meets the other four obligations and misses a reached store.
At the domain level, Goblint once claimed c % 2 == 1 for c ∈ {−5, −7}, although C’s
truncating remainder gives −1 (issue 1156, fixed in [27]). The defect is in a domain, not
in contexts. Every Voblint domain’s remainder must meet mod_sound of the locale sound_
arith_ops (the truncating remainder of concrete values lies in the concretization of the
abstract one), which the shipped congruence remainder discharges by congruence_mod_
sound. prefix_congruence_mod_unsound proves that every abstract remainder returning
the constant 1 for (1 + 2Z) mod 2 violates the obligation at −5, so the defective operator
could not be registered.

Contexts decide checks on recursive programs (executable). Table 3 runs one regression
program under every context policy. Mutually recursive procedures f and g return 2 and
1 at the bottom of the recursion. main calls f(2) and g(2) inside a loop of 200 iterations
and checks both results. Each call chain is three call sites deep. The table also counts the
copies of f and g the analysis creates. With one context per procedure, or with call strings

the loop counter. For a related program, the theories prove that the solution of the routed

length 1 (sign_k2_strictly_more_precise_than_k1_at_g). That theorem compares two
solutions computed by eval. It concerns neither run_voblint nor call strings in general.

        8     Related Work

       Concrete semantics of calling contexts. Sharir and Pnueli distinguish the functional
       approach, which computes input–output relations of procedures, from the call-string approach,
       which tags information with the history of calls [32]. Voblint’s call-string policy follows
       the second, and its entry-state policy, which indexes a callee’s exit states by its entry
       state, resembles a functional approach tabulated per input. Knoop and Steffen handle the
       locals of recursive procedures with stacks of lattice elements, of which the maximal-fixpoint
       strategy needs only two [16]; Voblint’s return has the shape of their return function [16,
       §4.1]. Reps et al. solve such problems precisely when facts are finite and transfer functions
       distributive [25]. Voblint requires neither and proves no precision result. Jeannet and
       Serwe derive interprocedural analyses by abstract interpretation of a standard stack-based
       operational semantics and combine the call-string and functional approaches [14]. Van Horn
       and Might derive sound analyses from abstract machines by bounding their stores [12]. These
M. Lerchner                                                                                             1:15


give contexts a concrete counterpart on paper. In the a posteriori soundness of Might and
Manolios [21], the state abstraction is fixed in advance and a map from concrete to abstract
contexts is chosen per run, after the analysis, from non-empty candidate sets. Voblint’s
entry-state policy instead reads a single relation off the solution, with Total in the role of
non-emptiness (Section 4).

Verified analyzers. Verasco is a Coq-verified analyzer for most of C99, excluding recursion
and dynamic allocation [15]. Its interpreter iterates over the structure of C#minor, reanalyzes
a function at every call site, and raises an alarm at a possible recursive call. Blazy et al. check
the candidate of an untrusted iterator with a verified checker [3]. Nipkow and Klein develop
abstract interpretation for IMP in Isabelle/HOL [23, 24], and Voblint’s non-relational domains
follow their generic interpreter. Their language has no procedures. Franceschino et al. [9]
and Cachera and Pichardie [6] verify executable analyzers for languages without procedures.
Michelland et al. make soundness proofs reusable by sharing one interpreter between the
concrete and the abstract semantics [20]. Cachera et al. compute a context-insensitive
dataflow analysis with a verified solver for ordinary inequations [5].

Contexts in mechanized semantics. Dabrowski and Pichardie instrument a concrete
semantics with contexts through a module type whose call-context function computes a
callee’s context, and prove a context-sensitive points-to analysis sound in Coq [7]. This is the
closest precedent for Voblint’s context-indexed semantics. Their analyses are given only as
constraints that no solver computes [7, §7]. Voblint’s contexts are admitted by a relation, the
totality condition makes the indexed collection exhaustive, and the indexing is connected to
an executable verified solver. Trace partitioning indexes sets of traces by control history [26];
Rival and Mauborgne allow coverings in which one trace has several indices and present
calling contexts as one such indexing. The activation collecting semantics is a covering of
this relational kind. Among Isabelle developments, Lammich and Müller-Olm verify the
soundness and precision of a conflict analysis for programs with procedures and threads [17],
Wasserrab verifies an interprocedural slicer [37], and Breitner formalizes Shivers’ control-flow
analysis for functional programs [4]. None of these entries claims an extracted analyzer or
represents an activation with its caller chain.

Verified solvers. Hofmann et al. verify a local generic solver in Coq [11], and de Vilhena
et al. verify one that discovers dependencies dynamically in Iris [8]. Stade et al. prove
the top-down solver partially correct in Isabelle/HOL [33], and Tilscher et al. extend it
to side effects with a generic interface for update rules [34]. Voblint vendors the latter.
Their termination result for top-down solvers without side effects assumes a finite type of
unknowns [35], which Voblint’s node and context types are not.

Goblint and local traces. Side-effecting constraint systems [30, 1] and the split between
analyses and solvers are Goblint’s architecture [36, 31], and Voblint’s dg_spec follows its Spec
interface. No theorem transfers to Goblint’s OCaml implementation. Schwarz et al. prove
Goblint’s thread-modular analyses sound against local traces on paper [28, 29]; activation
traces adapt the construction, but no concurrency result transfers.

Differences from prior work. Voblint inherits its solver, the side-effecting constraint systems,
the analysis interface, the shape of its traces, and the distinction between call-string and
functional contexts. What it adds is their mechanized connection, ending in a theorem about



                                                                                                      ITP 2027
1:16   End-to-End Soundness of a Goblint-Style Analyzer


       the exported analyzer; we know of no prior mechanization of it. Dabrowski and Pichardie [7]
       have contexts in the semantics but no solver. Verasco [15] covers a far larger language but
       reanalyzes functions per call site, raises an alarm at recursion, and iterates structurally
       instead of using a generic solver.


        9     Conclusion
       We have mechanized the connection between a source semantics and a Goblint-style, context-
       sensitive, side-effecting equation system solved by a verified solver, with activation traces as
       the concrete counterpart of contexts. This is a first step. The result is partial correctness,
       VIMP is scalar, the guarantee is per program point, and precision is shown only by witnesses
       for single programs.

       Per-context certification. Theorem 2 bounds each context by its solved state, but the
       report-level theorem keeps only the union over contexts. Proving that the report’s row for a
       context covers A(v, c) would certify the per-context states.

       Memory. The contract refers to the language only through a few step functions, so features
       such as machine integers leave it intact. The trace semantics, however, assumes that a
       callee cannot modify its caller’s locals. Pointers into stack frames and threads break this
       assumption; a heap without such pointers needs only a richer store model.

       Termination. Known termination results for top-down solvers assume finitely many un-
       knowns [35]. Entry-state contexts need not be finite, and all timeouts in Section 7 come
       from them. Goblint bounds them with optional widening on contexts (ana.context.widen)
       or a context gas; a general result would need such a bound in the routing.

       Data availability. The Isabelle/HOL development and the regression corpus are public on
       GitHub, and an earlier version is archived on Zenodo [18]. That archive lacks the vendored
       solver, the claims and profiling data behind Tables 3 and 4, and the scripts that render the
       paper’s tables; they will be released together with this paper. The vendored solver [34] is
       not yet public; it is planned for publication in the Archive of Formal Proofs. Until then the
       theories cannot be built from public sources alone.

       Declaration about GenAI/LLM use. Generative AI tools (Anthropic’s Claude, OpenAI’s
       ChatGPT and Codex, and Cursor) were used substantially in this work and contributed a
       large share of the implementation-level material under the author’s supervision. In the Isabelle
       development, they generated many proofs once definitions and interfaces had stabilized,
       carried out refactorings across theories, and turned tactic-style proofs into structured Isar.
       They also assisted with design: they helped adapt the local traces of Schwarz et al. to
       procedure activations, and the requirements on activation traces were refined in collaboration
       with them. Agents wrote much of the surrounding tooling, including the command-line
       interface, the regression corpus, and the consistency checks. The author chose the research
       direction, selected and revised the designs, and approved the definitions and theorem
       statements; Isabelle checked every proof. The tools also assisted with literature search and
       with drafting and revising this text and its figures, which the author reviewed. Grammarly
       was used for spelling and grammar.
