### Seidl and Fecht, JLP 2000 (supplied full text)

Bibliographic data, Crossref: Helmut Seidl, Christian Fecht, "Interprocedural analyses: a comparison", *The Journal of Logic Programming* 43(2):123–156, May 2000, DOI [10.1016/S0743-1066(99)00058-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0743-1066(99)00058-8). This matches `thesis/literature.bib` `seidl00`.

What the paper contains, with page locators from the supplied text:

- **§4 (pp. 128–129).** The operational semantics is a PDA whose configurations are a state plus a stack of activation records (d_j, e_j) for "called, but not yet completed procedures". The transitions are Push (call: Entry_e, and the caller's state stays stacked), Pop (Exit_r), Shift (intraprocedural) and Unpack, which computes Comb_e(d₁, d₂) from the stacked caller state and the callee's result. A return is split into Pop followed by Unpack "to distinguish between the effects of procedures and the effects of calls". §4.1 (p. 129) says the approach fails for threads communicating through shared variables. §4.2 (p. 130) notes that path properties can be handled by an instrumented semantics recording history in the state.
- **§5, Theorem 2 (pp. 130–131).** A concrete PDA is simulated by an abstract PDA through a left-total relation Δ. Reachability, effects and same-level reachability transfer.
- **§6 (pp. 131–133).** "A configuration is abstracted by recording the k+1 topmost stack elements": call strings as a stack abstraction. The functional approach introduces one unknown y_{f,d} per transformer application, so that the least solution "needs to be only partially computed. This idea is exploited by local solvers."
- **§7.2, system F_f (p. 135).** Unknowns ⟨v, d⟩ hold same-level reachability at node v for entry value d. The call inequation is ⟨v, d⟩ ⊒ Comb_e(p(Entry_e⟨u, d⟩), ⟨u, d⟩).
- **§9.1, proviso (R) and Theorems 7 and 9 (pp. 138–139).** Proviso (R) requires every node to reach a return point. Then, for *any* solution σ of F_f (not necessarily least), every state reaching v in p is below σ⟨v, d₁⟩ for some p d₁ ∈ dep({main d₀}, σ). Pp. 139–140 add that a local solver's domain can replace the dependence set, because under (R) a local solver includes all ⟨v, d⟩ once p d is included. Reachability per point is collected "as a side effect" into ⟨v⟩.

How this relates to Voblint:

1. **Activation traces vs. the PDA.** Voblint's graph semantics with a frame stack is the S–F PDA. Activation traces regroup it per activation. `Call t π` corresponds to Push, and `Resume t′ t″ π` to Pop followed by Unpack: `combine e (sink_store t′, sink_store t″)` is Comb_e(d₁, d₂), with d₁ the stacked caller state. The difference is the representation. S–F keep the stacked records in the configuration; Voblint recovers the creating caller through `caller_of`. That change lets contexts stay out of the semantics. The adaptation of this structure from Schwarz et al.'s local traces remains the paper's design. S–F should be cited as the prior PDA formulation it regroups.
2. **Context policies.** S–F obtain call strings as an abstraction of PDA configurations (top k stack symbols). Voblint reads the same information from the caller chain through a relation, `adm`. S–F have no relational, solution-dependent policy, and their soundness (Thm 2) is an a priori simulation. The entry-state policy `routed_entry_context_rel` (`Routed_Context.thy:703`) has no counterpart there.
3. **Context-indexed unknowns.** Voblint's entry-state unknowns (v, c), with c derived from the routed entry state, are S–F's ⟨v, d⟩ of F_f. The call contribution of §4.2 (publish d to seed(p, c′); read (FunctionResult p, c′); combine(q, r)) is F_f's call inequation with the entry passed through a side-effected seed unknown. Call-string unknowns have no counterpart in F_f. S–F treat call strings only as the alternative they set aside (p. 132).
4. **Solver bridge (§4.3).** This is the significant overlap. `live_unknowns` (`DG_Live_Unknowns.thy:230`) consists of the solved (u, c) whose procedure result (FunctionResult q, c) is solved. `live_unknowns_cover` derives entry membership and forward closure from closure under reads plus "the compiler ends every body with a return edge". That is S–F's proviso (R) and their observation on pp. 139–140, mechanized and extended to side effects, global unknowns and routing. The per-point guarantee of Theorem 3 also has the same shape as S–F Thm 9(2): the bound at v is a join over the entry values/contexts in the dependence set, not a per-context row. §4.3 currently presents the argument without attribution.
5. **Limits of the correspondence.** S–F work on paper, use one value per procedure input, and assume monotone, total behavioural functions on a complete lattice for F_f (p. 135). They have no global unknowns or side-effecting solver beyond the ⟨v⟩ accumulation, no widening solver, and no source-to-CFG link. Voblint's mechanized composition with the verified side-effecting TD solver and the exported analyzer remains new relative to S–F.

**Proposed sentences.**

- §1, after "Such semantics are classical on paper [32, 16, 14, 12]": add [SF00]. Then append: "Seidl and Fecht give one as a pushdown automaton over stacked activation records and relate it to call-string and functional equation systems [SF00, §§4–7]."
- §4.3, after "This is structural reachability in the graph.": "The argument is a mechanized form of Seidl and Fecht's extraction of reachability from the variable dependences of a functional system: their proviso that every program point reaches a return point is the property of the compiler used here, and they observe that a local solver's domain may replace the dependence set [SF00, §9.1, Thm. 9]. We add contexts chosen by routing, side effects to global unknowns, and the solver's partial post-solution certificate."
- §8, "Concrete semantics of calling contexts", after the Sharir–Pnueli sentence: "Seidl and Fecht model the small-step semantics of programs with procedures as a pushdown automaton whose stack holds activation records, obtain call strings by abstracting a configuration to its topmost stack symbols, and represent the functional approach by unknowns ⟨v, d⟩ for a program point and an entry value, which a local solver computes only on demand [SF00, §§4–6, §7.2]. Activation traces regroup that automaton by activation: Call corresponds to its push transition and Resume to pop followed by unpack, and Voblint's entry-state unknowns are of the form ⟨v, d⟩. Their soundness argument is an a priori simulation; Voblint's entry-state policy is read off the solution."
- §8, Differences paragraph, inherited list: "… the distinction between call-string and functional contexts, and the reading of reachability off the solved set of a local solver [SF00]."

Citing S–F correctly, in §4.3 as well as §8, resolves my repeated request. A citation only in §8 would leave the §4.3 overlap unattributed.
