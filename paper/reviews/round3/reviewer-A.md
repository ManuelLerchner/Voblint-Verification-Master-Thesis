# Reviewer A

## Summary

The paper connects a source semantics for a scalar imperative language with recursive procedures to a context-sensitive, side-effecting equation system and an existing verified solver. Its contribution is the composition: activation traces give contexts a concrete interpretation; five coverage obligations support a reusable soundness argument; routing connects that argument to equations; and a compiler simulation and report interpretation produce a theorem about the exported analyzer. The proof-engineering content goes beyond implementing a familiar analysis. In particular, the caller-chain induction, totality requirement, and conversion of backward dependency closure to forward execution coverage address genuine compositional difficulties. I checked the current submission against the supplied source, without building Isabelle. I found no contradiction between the central soundness statements and their formal counterparts. The principal revisions concern one incomplete hypothesis description, a compressed part of the solver bridge, and reproducibility of the published artifact.

## Recommendation

**Minor revision. Confidence: 4/5.**

This recommendation concerns the technical paper and the locally supplied sources. A complete, versioned artifact containing the solver dependency should be available before publication. I did not independently establish successful batch checking, the absence of admissions throughout the development, or the empirical measurements. The source checks below establish statement correspondence and inspect proof structure, not a fresh kernel verification.

## Strengths

- **A specific mechanization contribution.** Sections 3.3 and 4.3, pp. 6 and 9, identify why the natural induction and the solver certificate do not immediately suffice. Recovering the caller's invariant through the caller chain and establishing forward coverage from backward reads are stronger evidence of proof design than the size table.
- **Useful separation of obligations.** Sections 3–5 distinguish the concrete collecting semantics, a policy-dependent indexing, abstract transfer laws, equation bounds, and report semantics. Section 4.2, p. 8, explains the solution-dependent entry-state relation without suggesting that defining a policy from a solution proves soundness by itself. Totality and the policy-independent collection remain explicit.
- **Faithful treatment of scope.** Section 5, p. 10, explicitly limits the final result to the union over report contexts. Section 5, p. 11, identifies the handwritten trace hook and differences from C. These qualifications materially affect what the theorem establishes.
- **Design alternatives have technical content.** Section 6, pp. 11–12, compares activation traces with a ghost-context frame stack, distinguishes the abandoned prototype from an unimplemented alternative, and explains why contexts form a relation. The solver locale also gives a concrete reusable boundary. The alternatives are not experimentally compared, but the paper does not claim they are.

## Major weaknesses

1. **The public artifact does not yet support independent checking of the complete claim.** Section 9, p. 16, states: “The vendored solver … is not yet public, so the theories cannot yet be built from public sources alone.” It also states that the archive predates some claims and profiling data. For a mechanization paper, this prevents a reader from reproducing the principal evidence using the cited release. This is a release problem, not a demonstrated defect in the proofs I inspected. Provide a complete frozen artifact, including the exact modified solver revision or an authorized bundled copy, matching the paper's claims and scripts. Identify the build command and dependency versions. If distribution cannot be resolved, this becomes a substantive obstacle to acceptance rather than a minor finalization task.

## Minor comments

1. **Table 1, p. 8, understates the central post-solution hypothesis.** The `pp (post_bounded)` row reads “σ bounds every right-hand side on the solved set V.” It must also bound every emitted side effect at its target. The formal `post_bounded` uses `tree_covered_at` (`vendor/td-verification/Basics_side.thy:619`), and the framework explicitly extracts separate local and side-effect bounds in `src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Activation/DG_Ctx_Activation.thy:97` and `:101`. The distinction is essential to the seed publication argument. Section 4.3 subsequently states both bounds correctly, so this looks like an incomplete table entry, not a false formal theorem. State both inequalities in Table 1 or immediately before Theorem 2.

2. **Section 4.3, p. 9: make the callee part of the backward-to-forward bridge explicit.** The paragraph explains why a solved procedure result pulls in the nodes of that procedure, but “live_unknowns_cover proves … closed forward” compresses the additional argument needed to enter another procedure. The source uses the solved continuation's dependency on the callee result, conditional on a non-bottom entry, and then walks backwards inside the callee (`src/Analyses/Shared/Result/DG_Live_Unknowns.thy:181`, `:291`). Add two sentences connecting continuation, callee-result read, and callee-entry coverage. This would expose the role of dynamic dependencies and bottom filtering in a central proof step, without reproducing Isabelle syntax.

3. **Section 4.2, p. 7: distinguish mathematical routing shorthand from the interface.** The prose says routing uses “the call site, the caller context, and the entry state,” and the displayed entry-state policy uses `route(u,c,e′)`. The formal route also receives the call action (`src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Activation/DG_Ctx_Activation.thy:32`; `src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Context/Routed_Context.thy:107`). Since the policy already fixes a call, suppressing that parameter is reasonable, but say it is suppressed. Otherwise the claimed generic interface appears narrower than the one verified.

4. **Section 6, p. 12: soften the general claim about code generation.** “Code generation turns finite sets and maps into lists that every lookup scans” describes the current representation setup, not an intrinsic property of Isabelle code generation. The paragraph itself later identifies alternative code setups. Say “Our current code setup represents finite sets and maps by lists.” This aligns the tooling feedback with the actual implementation choice and leaves the reported profiling observation intact.

5. **Section 9, p. 16: qualify the shared-heap limitation.** “Pointers into the caller’s frame, a shared heap, or threads break this assumption” groups distinct extensions together. Pointers into caller locals directly violate restoration of those locals; a shared heap represented as global state need not do so by itself. The present concrete return operation is store-based (`src/Program_Model/CFG/CFG_Transfer.thy:52`), with global components taken from the callee (`src/Program_Model/VIMP/VIMP_Globals.thy:22`). A heap extension would still need a model and sound transfers, but the sentence should identify the feature that invalidates preservation of caller locals. Treat this as a boundary of the current representation rather than a general impossibility for activation traces.

## Questions for the author

1. Can the full solver dependency and the exact paper evidence be archived with the revision, so that a reader can rebuild the end-to-end theorem without private access?
2. In Section 4.3, is the dynamic callee-result dependency argument intended to cover the generic multi-alternative `enter` interface, or only the concrete singleton-entry analyses used by the executable? A short statement of scope would separate the generic coverage theorem from its concrete solver-closure instantiations.
3. Does the per-context reporting gap in Sections 5 and 9 require only a correspondence theorem about row construction, or are there cases where row merging or context serialization changes the intended index? Explaining the expected remaining obligation would help readers judge this limitation without requiring an additional theorem for the present paper.
