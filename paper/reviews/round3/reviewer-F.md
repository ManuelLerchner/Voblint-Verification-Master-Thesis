# Summary

The paper connects source execution, activation traces, context-indexed coverage, side-effecting equations, a previously verified solver, and the exported analyzer's verdicts. I tried to reject it on the ground that choosing entry-state contexts after solving makes soundness circular, especially since the final report theorem forgets contexts. That argument fails: the concrete collecting semantics is independent of the solution, totality prevents the policy from discarding reached calls, and the source theorem concludes coverage for actual source executions. The missing certification of individual report rows is a real limitation, but the paper states it explicitly. I found no fatal semantic defect, vacuity, or established equivalent prior result in the material inspected.

## Recommendation

**Minor revision. Confidence: 4/5.** This assesses the scientific argument. A complete, matching, redistributable artifact should be a condition of final publication. Inspection was read-only; I did not rebuild Isabelle. Source references below identify inspected definitions and statements, not independently rerun proof checks.

## Strengths

- The strongest circularity objection is answered by a substantive obligation. Section 3.3, p. 6, requires nonempty admission on covered calls and explains its use at returns. The routed locale makes this an explicit hypothesis (`src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Context/Routed_Context.thy:104`), derives the coverage contract's totality (`:566`), and proves the indexed bound (`:585`). The policy cannot simply hide an inconvenient reached activation.
- The end-to-end statement does not assume the desired coverage. Section 5, p. 10, Theorem 3 has a source run and a returned report as premises and includes existence of an observed node. This matches `src/Executable_Surface/CLI/Analysis_Certified.thy:196`. The non-vacuity witness additionally combines a concrete returning execution with two definite verdicts (`src/Examples/Capstone/Example_Non_Vacuity.thy:151`), rather than merely exhibiting a well-formed program.
- The novelty claim is appropriately about composition. Section 8, p. 15, identifies a much closer predecessor than verified intraprocedural IMP analyses. I checked Frédéric Dabrowski and David Pichardie, *A Certified Data Race Analysis for a Java-like Language*, TPHOLs 2009, LNCS 5674, pp. 212–227, DOI [10.1007/978-3-642-03359-9_16](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-03359-9_16), against the [author-hosted paper](https://davidpichardie.github.io/papers/tphols09.pdf). Its Section 4 gives context-parameterized semantics and constraint-solution soundness. That establishes substantial prior overlap, but does not establish equivalence to this paper's connection to an executable verified side-effecting solver. A broader priority claim remains UNVERIFIED by this limited review.

## Major weaknesses

1. **The public artifact does not yet support the submitted version.** Section 9, p. 16, says the archive “predates the context-policy claims and the profiling data” and that the vendored solver “is not yet public.” Thus the public references do not currently provide a reproducible basis for the central composition or all evaluation claims. Local reviewer access to sources does not resolve the publication issue. Supply an immutable archive matching the paper, including the exact modified solver, its redistribution terms, generated-output inputs, and benchmark records. This is a release requirement, not evidence that the theorem is false; I do not recommend rejection on this basis if it is resolved before publication. Whether the missing solver can legally be redistributed is **UNVERIFIED** here.

2. **The title's context-sensitive guarantee ends before individual report rows.** Section 5, p. 10, states: “No theorem about run_voblint certifies the row of an individual context.” The source confirms that `context_sound` bounds each activation bucket by the union report semantics, not by the corresponding row (`src/Executable_Surface/CLI/Analysis_Report.thy:517`). Thus a context-label or row-association error could escape that predicate while node-level verdict soundness remains intact. My attempted title/content rejection nevertheless fails: a context-sensitive computation can have a sound context-erased public guarantee, Theorem 2 separately supplies the context-indexed bound, and the limitation is explicit in the introduction and conclusion. Keep this boundary explicit in the abstract as well: one short statement that individual context rows are outside the report theorem would prevent readers from interpreting “for every configuration” as a stronger output guarantee. I do not require the additional row theorem for acceptance of the stated result.

## Minor comments

1. **Section 4.3, p. 9, “Closure under reads then puts all nodes of a procedure into V.”** This bridge is one of the most interesting parts of the contribution, but receives less explanation than the five elementary coverage laws. Add a small dependency diagram or a few sentences showing why the backward demand from a procedure result includes a call before a nonreturning execution. Distinguish structural access to the continuation from actual completion of the callee. The current text already makes the distinction verbally; a concrete example would make its force easier to assess.

2. **Section 7, p. 14, running-time paragraph.** The text gives three repetitions, 5,535 fixture/configuration cases, percentile times, and 21 cases exceeding the limit. State whether the percentiles summarize individual repetitions or per-case aggregates, how repeated timeouts are aggregated, and whether censored cases are excluded. These details determine what the reported median and percentile mean; no additional benchmark campaign is needed.

3. **Section 9, p. 16, “Pointers into the caller's frame, a shared heap, or threads break this assumption.”** The preceding assumption is that a callee cannot change its caller's locals. A shared heap without pointers into caller-local storage need not violate that assumption. Separate features requiring a richer store/transfer model from features invalidating restoration of the caller's locals. Whether the present formalization can accommodate a particular heap extension unchanged is **UNVERIFIED**, and the revision should avoid promising that.

4. **Front matter, p. 1.** The frozen submission uses ITP 2027 proceedings metadata, placeholder editors, and a proceedings DOI. For a JAR submission, replace these with the correct journal submission metadata; this is a formatting mismatch, not a mismatch between the scientific title and the result.

## Questions for the author

- Can the complete exact solver dependency and the paper's current generated evidence be archived before final publication? What prevents doing so now? (Section 9, p. 16.)
- Is the missing context-row theorem only a missing preservation lemma for report construction, or does the report representation merge or rename contexts in a way that needs a revised statement? (Section 5, p. 10; `src/Executable_Surface/CLI/Analysis_Report.thy:517`.)
- Which concrete dependency property ensures the live-set bridge retains prefix states of a nonreturning recursive activation, and can the exposition illustrate it directly? (Section 4.3, p. 9; theorem located at `src/Analyses/Shared/Result/DG_Live_Unknowns.thy:251`.)

**Devil's-advocate conclusion:** the strongest scientific rejection argument—post-hoc contexts plus context-erased output make the central result circular or empty—does **not** survive scrutiny. The solution-dependent partition must still cover a solution-independent concrete semantics, and the source endpoint and non-vacuity witness impose observable obligations. The surviving concerns concern artifact availability, the scope of certified output, and exposition; they do not establish that the stated theorem is trivial or false.
