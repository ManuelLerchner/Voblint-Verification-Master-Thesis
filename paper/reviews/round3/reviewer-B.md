# Summary

The paper connects a small imperative source language with recursive procedures to a context-sensitive, side-effecting equation system and an executable Isabelle/HOL analyzer. Its semantic intermediate representation consists of activation traces with caller links. A relational context policy indexes these traces; five coverage obligations imply soundness of the indexed collecting semantics. A second theorem discharges these obligations from a post-solution and routing assumptions. A bridge from the existing solver certificate to forward coverage, followed by source simulation and report construction, yields a per-program-point theorem about the exported analyzer. The contribution is the mechanized composition and its interfaces. Calling contexts, entry-state tabulation, trace coverings, and generic solvers are established techniques. The paper mostly draws this boundary accurately. Its central technical value is making the dependencies between those techniques explicit enough to prove their composition, including recursion and a solution-dependent context policy.

## Recommendation and confidence

**Major revision. Confidence: 3/5.**

This is a substantial contribution suitable in subject and ambition for JAR, with a convincing proof architecture. For a journal article, I recommend a revision that makes the context-specific contribution concrete, exposes the context-policy evaluation, and supplies the exact reproducible development before acceptance. These requests concern the completeness of the present account and evidence; I do not require a new analysis algorithm or a general precision theorem. The paper gives useful reasons why assembling existing components requires additional invariants, especially caller-chain closure and the backward-certificate/forward-coverage bridge. My reservations concern the strength of the delivered context guarantee, the evaluation of context policies, and the currently unavailable reproducible artifact. I read the complete 19-page submission text and the related-work and evaluation LaTeX. I checked relevant primary literature online; I did not replay Isabelle proofs or audit the implementation. No source-level defect is alleged below.

## Strengths

- The semantic, analysis, routing, and solver obligations are separated clearly. Table 1 makes the assumptions of the second theorem inspectable instead of concealing them behind an informal soundness claim.
- The return proof explains why a single-trace induction invariant fails and why closure along the creating caller chain repairs it. The solver bridge explains a second real difficulty: dependency closure of a backward query is not immediately forward execution coverage.
- Relational admission and Total are treated carefully. In particular, the solution-dependent entry-state policy is not presented as a fixed semantic partition, and the paper explains why fixing the solution before defining the relation avoids circularity.
- The end-to-end statement covers intermediate configurations of finite execution prefixes, not just terminating source executions. The distinctions between PROVED, REFUTED, DEAD, partial correctness, and trusted execution machinery are unusually explicit.
- The mechanization is instantiated with several domains, several context choices, and all offered solver update rules. The non-vacuity examples and the counterexample after removing Total help establish that the interfaces have substantive content.

## Major weaknesses

1. **The delivered theorem loses the context-specific meaning that motivates the construction.** Section 5 explicitly says that the report theorem bounds each context's collection only by the union of all reported states at the node. Thus it does not certify the interpretation a reader naturally assigns to an individual context row in Figure 1. This is disclosed, so I do not regard the theorem as misleadingly stated or unsound. It nevertheless leaves an important final link unfinished for a paper whose distinguishing subject is context sensitivity. Please explain whether the missing result is a bookkeeping theorem about report construction or whether context identities are transformed or forgotten. Ideally provide the row-specific theorem; otherwise distinguish the certified node verdicts from the illustrative context rows directly in the figure caption and explain the obstacle in more detail.

2. **The context evaluation does not expose the precision/cost tradeoff.** Table 3 establishes one useful recursive precision witness, but reports neither context counts nor solver work. The later aggregate timing numbers pool 15 domain/context combinations, hiding the very distinction the paper asks readers to understand. The 21 timeouts also need a breakdown by policy and domain. A compact table for the recursive example should show contexts, solved unknowns or evaluations, runtime, and verdicts for unit, k=1, k=2, and entry-state policies. Add a second example where entry-state sensitivity costs substantially more or fails to terminate within the limit. The existing single-program theorem is correctly qualified; no general monotonicity theorem is necessary. The requested evidence would show the practical consequences of the proved interfaces without turning this into a performance paper.

3. **The distinction from existing semantic frameworks is accurate but insufficiently operational.** Sections 3 and 8 acknowledge the relevant predecessors, yet the reader sees only a named witness for overlapping admission. Show a small call whose enter alternatives overlap, identify the two admitted contexts, and explain exactly why universal coverage of every admitted context and Total are both needed. This would distinguish the mechanized obligation from the familiar observation that histories can index a covering. Jeannet and Serwe already separate stack abstraction from data abstraction; Dabrowski and Pichardie already parameterize a mechanized semantics by method contexts and prove constraint-solution soundness. The paper's real increment is connecting such indexing to this executable solver and source/report pipeline. Make that increment concrete rather than adding a broader novelty claim.

   Primary sources checked: Bertrand Jeannet and Wendelin Serwe, *Abstracting Call-Stacks for Interprocedural Verification of Imperative Programs*, AMAST 2004, LNCS 3116, pp. 258–273, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-27815-3_22, especially Sections 1 and 3–5 ([author PDF](https://pop-art.inrialpes.fr/people/bjeannet/publications/amast04.pdf)); Frédéric Dabrowski and David Pichardie, *A Certified Data Race Analysis for a Java-like Language*, TPHOLs 2009, LNCS 5674, pp. 212–227, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-03359-9_16, especially Section 4 and Figure 6 ([author PDF](https://davidpichardie.github.io/papers/tphols09.pdf)). These works support the comparison; they do not establish that the present complete pipeline was previously mechanized.

4. **The public artifact cannot currently substantiate the mechanization claim independently.** Section 9 states that the vendored solver is not public and that the archive predates some evidence used in the paper. This is an explicit limitation, not a hidden omission, but reproducibility matters particularly when the contribution is the checked composition of components. Supply an accessible review artifact containing the precise solver revision, local changes, Isabelle version, and the commands that regenerate the paper's claims. A future camera-ready promise leaves the present review unable to test the central deliverable. I have not treated the local existence of sources as evidence that the public package builds.

## Minor comments

1. Section 9 groups a shared heap with pointers into a caller's frame and threads as necessarily breaking preservation of caller locals. That is too broad: a heap threaded as shared state need not permit modification of caller-local bindings. Jeannet and Serwe explicitly describe modelling a heap through an extra procedure parameter on p. 261 of the primary source cited above. Separate the need to enrich stores/transfers from the stronger need to change activation/return structure. I am questioning the general limitation claim, not asserting that this implementation already supports heaps.

2. The account of entry-state contexts as resembling tabulated functional analysis is appropriately cautious. A useful additional reference is Helmut Seidl and Christian Fecht, *Interprocedural Analyses: A Comparison*, Journal of Logic Programming 43(2), 2000, pp. 123–156, DOI 10.1016/S0743-1066(99)00058-8 ([publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0743106699000588)). Its primary abstract confirms a small-step framework comparing relational/functional approaches and accumulation strategies. **UNVERIFIED:** I did not inspect its full technical development, so I do not claim that it subsumes Voblint's precise equations or obligations. Its omission does not independently weaken the mechanization novelty.

3. Keep the limited analogy to a posteriori soundness. Matthew Might and Panagiotis Manolios, *A Posteriori Soundness for Non-deterministic Abstract Interpretations*, VMCAI 2009, LNCS 5403, pp. 260–274, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-93900-9_22 ([author publication page](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/~pete/research/aposteriori-soundness.html)), construct justifying abstraction maps after an analysis. The primary abstract supports this distinction. **UNVERIFIED:** I could not inspect the full PDF; I make no stronger equivalence claim. Similarly, citing AAM does not require claiming that Voblint implements higher-order k-CFA.

4. State which 15 domain/context combinations were timed, how three repetitions were aggregated, and whether timeout cases were excluded from the reported quantiles. The present timing paragraph gives a case count but not the statistical unit used for the summaries.

5. The Monniaux examples provide useful external selection, but the paper should identify the eight checks and their five successful verdicts in a small table or stable artifact manifest. Referring readers to a thesis for the actual comparison weakens this otherwise useful evaluation component.

## Questions for the authors

- What precise additional lemma would turn Theorem 2 into a certified individual context row of run_voblint's report?
- Do any shipped analyses actually produce overlapping enter alternatives, or is the relational case exercised only by a dedicated witness? What would the overlap example look like in the paper's notation?
- How many contexts and solved unknowns do the four policies create on Table 3's program, and which configurations account for the 21 timeouts?
- Can the exact vendored solver and the current evidence archive be made available during review?
