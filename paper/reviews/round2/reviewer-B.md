# Reviewer B

## Summary

The paper connects source executions of a small recursive imperative language to a context-indexed collecting semantics, side-effecting equations, a reused verified solver, and the reported analyzer verdicts. Activation traces retain the suspended caller and completed callee; a relational context policy supports overlapping alternatives. The strongest contribution is the mechanized composition of these components, including coverage of the solver's discovered unknowns. The submission does not yet establish the conceptual novelty of its treatment of contexts against the relevant semantic literature, and it gives too little mathematical detail about the result-dependent policy that distinguishes its construction. Its evaluation exercises contexts, but the main comparison table evaluates numeric abstractions rather than the central interprocedural mechanism.

## Recommendation

**Major revision. Confidence: 4/5.**

Assessment uses the frozen `reviews/round2/submission.txt`, the corresponding section sources, selected formal sources, primary literature, and two CLI executions. I did not independently rebuild Isabelle. I found no demonstrated soundness counterexample; the recommendation concerns positioning, exposition of the central construction, and evidence supporting its scope.

## Strengths

- The coverage argument establishes both inclusion in each admitted context and existence of an admitted context. This makes the transition back to an unindexed source guarantee substantive. See §3.3, pp. 5–6; `src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Activation/Activation_Backbone.thy:34–48` and `src/Program_Model/CFG/Collecting/Activation_Trace_Abstract.thy:247–277`.
- Relational admission has an actual overlapping witness. The theorem at `src/Examples/Sign/Example_Sign_DG_Overlapping_Enter.thy:1040–1046` admits the same concrete call under distinct `[SPos]` and `[STop]` contexts. Thus §3.2's reference to `ov_two_contexts_admitted` has more substance than merely observing two entries somewhere in a result table.
- The context precision witness is appropriately scoped. §7, p. 12 explicitly denies a general dominance theorem. The inequalities in `src/Examples/Sign/CallString/Example_Sign_DG_CallString_K2.thy:279–305` compare the two exact Sign entries against the merged entry. The paper also distinguishes Goblint architectural correspondence from correctness of Goblint's implementation (§8, p. 14).

## Major weaknesses

1. **The semantic novelty claim needs comparison with established accounts of contexts.** §1, p. 2 says a per-context claim has “nothing to be checked against” and lists “A concrete semantics of calling contexts” as its first contribution (`sections/introduction.tex:26–49`). §3 opens with “Neither a graph execution nor its flat list ... provides it” (`sections/traces.tex:4–12`). This is accurate for the particular store projection that has discarded context, but is too broad as motivation for a new semantic construction. The narrow integration claim at `sections/introduction.tex:66–71` is much more defensible. §8 compares mechanized analyzers and trace partitioning, but omits the following directly relevant foundations:

   - Micha Sharir and Amir Pnueli. **Two Approaches to Interprocedural Data Flow Analysis.** In Steven S. Muchnick and Neil D. Jones, eds., *Program Flow Analysis: Theory and Applications*, Prentice-Hall, 1981, pp. 189–233. The classical functional and call-string alternatives should locate the shipped entry-state and bounded-call-string policies. [Primary chapter scan](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~trentj/cse598-f11/docs/sharir_pnueli2.pdf).
   - Bertrand Jeannet and Wendelin Serwe. **Abstracting Call-Stacks for Interprocedural Verification of Imperative Programs.** AMAST 2004, LNCS 3116, pp. 258–273. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-540-27815-3_22. This derives interprocedural analyses from a standard operational semantics and abstracts stacks of activation records; its §§3–4 relate functional and call-string approaches. Compare the objects, caller information retained, and proof obligations with activation traces. [Author manuscript](https://pop-art.inrialpes.fr/people/bjeannet/publications/amast04.pdf).
   - Helmut Seidl and Christian Fecht. **Interprocedural Analyses: A Comparison.** *Journal of Logic Programming* 43(2), 2000, pp. 123–156. DOI: 10.1016/S0743-1066(99)00058-8. Its operational-semantics-based comparison of relational and functional analyses is relevant to the relationship between collecting semantics and equations. The publisher abstract verifies this scope; a stronger claim that it subsumes this particular contract is **UNVERIFIED** and is not asserted here. [Publisher record and abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0743106699000588).
   - David Van Horn and Matthew Might. **Abstracting Abstract Machines.** ICFP 2010, pp. 51–62. DOI: 10.1145/1863543.1863553. Sections 2.6–2.7 provide a soundness argument and a call-string-based, k-CFA-like instantiation. This is another precedent for making context sensitivity a parameter of a semantic soundness construction, although it is not the same side-effecting equation architecture. [Author manuscript](https://matt.might.net/papers/vanhorn2010abstract.pdf).

   These omissions do not disprove the claimed first mechanized connection to this solver. They prevent the reader from separating that contribution from familiar semantic context abstraction. Explain precisely what activation traces buy relative to stack-indexed collecting semantics and how the five obligations specialize general simulation/inductiveness conditions. Preserve the existing acknowledgment that relational coverings already occur in Rival–Mauborgne; listing that work alone does not supply the missing comparison.

2. **The result-dependent admission relation needs an explicit definition and proof explanation in the paper.** §4.2, p. 8 says the policy is “defined from the routing itself” and “may depend on the analysis result without circularity” (`sections/equations.tex:76–88`). This is central to the difference from a fixed call-context function, yet the paper gives only prose. The artifact has a concise definition: an alternative from the solved caller state must cover both the caller and entered stores, and its route determines the admitted context (`src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Context/Routed_Context.thy:703–713`). Totality then follows from entry-pair coverage (`:735–743`), while the routed locale separately requires the target to be solved (`:86–110`). Present this definition mathematically, with the solution parameter visible. Explain the quantifier order: fix a returned solution, construct its admission relation, discharge the coverage obligations, and eliminate the relation through the exhaustive union. Merely saying the final guarantee concerns an unchanged node collection leaves the decisive non-vacuity step implicit.

   Also compare **Matthew Might and Panagiotis Manolios, A Posteriori Soundness for Non-deterministic Abstract Interpretations**, VMCAI 2009, LNCS 5403, pp. 260–274, DOI: 10.1007/978-3-540-93900-9_22. This explicitly permits the justifying abstraction correspondence to be constructed after analysis. [Author manuscript](https://matt.might.net/papers/might2009aposteriori.pdf). The relationship is methodological; I am not claiming equivalence of the two frameworks. Explain whether Voblint's solved-table relation is a restricted instance of that idea or differs in its required correspondence and coverage conditions. The existing comparison with Dabrowski–Pichardie correctly identifies their functional context interface, but does not answer this question about result-dependent relations.

3. **The evaluation should report the behavior of the interprocedural mechanism it contributes.** §7, pp. 12–13 provides an entry-state example and one k=1/k=2 witness, followed by the flavors table over numeric domains and widening choices (`sections/evaluation.tex:13–79`). Runtime is pooled over 15 domain/context combinations (`:81–95`); neither the table nor the aggregate timing identifies context counts, precision by policy, or the configurations responsible for timeouts. Thus contexts are tested, but their behavior under recursion, merging, and shared global information is not made assessable in the paper. A small targeted table would suffice; a large benchmark campaign is unnecessary.

   The artifact already contains useful material. I ran `tests/regression/17-call-string/precision/09-depth2_mutual_recursion_in_loop.vimp` with the existing CLI, Interval, and call-string depth 2: both checks were `PROVED`, with results `[2,2]` and `[1,1]`. Running the same file with `--context none` produced two `UNKNOWN` checks, both `[1,12]`. The source at lines 12–30 shows the mutual calls and loop. This is executable evidence, not an additional Isabelle proof. Include examples of this kind, a recursive entry-state growth case, and the overlapping-enter witness with the actual alternatives and their coverage. Separate counts of contexts/unknowns, returned verdicts, and timeouts by policy. State which evidence uses the exported analyzer and which uses a specially instantiated theory-level analysis.

## Minor comments

1. §9, p. 14 says “No precision or completeness property is proved” (`sections/conclusion.tex:14`). §7 and the cited k=1/k=2 theorem prove a program-specific precision comparison. Change this to “No general precision ordering or completeness property is proved.”
2. §7, p. 13 reports 369 fixtures, 15 combinations, “three repetitions each,” and “21 of 5,535 runs” (`sections/evaluation.tex:84–89`). Since 369 × 15 = 5,535, explain whether “runs” means distinct configurations, whether timeouts are counted once per configuration, and how repetitions contribute to percentiles. As written, the sampling unit is unclear.
3. §8, p. 14 attributes non-applicability of a termination result partly to nodes having an infinite type (`sections/related.tex:46–48`). Distinguish this formal interface restriction from potentially unbounded generation of entry-state contexts: a finite program has finitely many syntactic nodes, and bounded call strings do not create the same issue as an unbounded abstract-value context space. This distinction would help interpret the timeout discussion without promising a termination theorem.
4. §4, Figure 2 says “Each box is proved without reference to the others” (`sections/equations.tex:29–30`). Routing adequacy explicitly mentions concretization, entry alternatives, the solution, and solved unknowns (`src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Context/Routed_Context.thy:86–110`). Say the components expose separate contracts and compose through them; literal independence overstates the modularity.

## Questions for the author

- Which property of activation traces is unavailable, or materially harder to state and mechanize, using a conventional collecting semantics over instrumented call stacks?
- Can the paper show the solved-table-dependent admission definition and the short argument deriving totality from entry-pair coverage, including an overlapping example?
- Which shipped configurations actually use overlapping entry alternatives? Which witnesses instantiate only the generic framework?
- Can existing regression results supply a context-policy table covering nested calls, recursion, globals, solved-context counts, and timeouts?
- Is the intended novelty specifically the mechanized source-to-side-effecting-solver composition? If so, can the title, first contribution, and related-work comparison consistently foreground that scope?
