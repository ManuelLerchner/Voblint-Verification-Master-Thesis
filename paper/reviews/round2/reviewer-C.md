# Reviewer C

## Summary

The paper connects source execution, activation traces, context-indexed collecting semantics, side-effecting equations, a certified solver, and report verdicts. The final theorem is unusually concrete: its quantified configuration is the argument of the exported analyzer function, and its conclusion covers finite execution prefixes at all observed nodes. Inspection of the Isabelle sources supports that endpoint and the stated termination and trust qualifications. My main concern is a mathematical mismatch in the presentation of the central coverage contract: its printed Return obligation is weaker than the Isabelle obligation on arbitrary graphs. The source development explicitly accommodates a case that the paper's simplified contract omits. This requires correction before acceptance; I found no corresponding defect in the implemented analyzer theorem.

## Strengths

- **The final statement matches the implementation.** Section 5, pp. 9–10, prints `run_voblint config p = Analysed res` as a premise and concludes existence of an observed node plus coverage at every observed node. `src/Executable_Surface/CLI/Analysis_Certified.thy:196` states precisely those premises and conclusions. The source theorem does not silently require a particular domain or context configuration.
- **The context partition is connected back to an independent semantics.** Section 4.2, p. 8, explicitly permits a result-dependent policy and says that the final guarantee concerns C. `src/Program_Model/CFG/Collecting/Activation_Trace_Collect.thy:31` defines C without a policy; `src/Program_Model/CFG/Collecting/Activation_Trace_Abstract.thy:268` proves `C v = (Union c. A v c)` under the coverage locale. This avoids treating a conveniently chosen context relation as sufficient evidence of soundness by itself.
- **The result interpretation is careful.** Section 5, p. 10, says that REFUTED is not a verified counterexample and identifies DEAD as the reachability claim. `src/Executable_Surface/CLI/Analysis_Certified.thy:116` requires membership in C before concluding falsity; line 129 gives emptiness of C for a Dead check. The text also distinguishes absent arithmetic diagnostics, definite arithmetic errors, and warnings.
- **Source adequacy and executable trust are explicitly separated.** Section 5, p. 10, acknowledges unbounded integers, total division, zero-initialized callee locals, and the unverified parser/printer. These are substantive qualifications, consistent with `src/Program_Model/VIMP/VIMP_Expr.thy:19`, `src/Program_Model/VIMP/VIMP_Globals.thy:41`, and the actual export of `run_voblint` at `src/Executable_Surface/Codegen/Voblint_Codegen.thy:135`.

## Major weaknesses

1. **The printed five-obligation contract is not the Isabelle contract for arbitrary graphs.**

   **Paper evidence.** The abstract, p. 1, claims “five local obligations over an arbitrary control-flow graph.” Section 3.3, pp. 5–6, introduces its formulas with “For a call edge from u into p with continuation k” and gives Return as admission of c′ for that call, membership in `cover(FunctionResult p,c′)`, and coverage of the combined store at k. The subsequent explanation says admission is “for the call from s in context c.” Read with the fixed call edge, this uses the same call to select both the context and return destination.

   **Isabelle evidence.** `src/Program_Model/CFG/Collecting/Activation_Trace_Abstract.thy:59` instead quantifies **both p and p′**. Its Return premise is `admits_call_context G g adm u c p' s es c'`, while the selected return edge enters p and the exit store lies in `cover (FunctionResult p) c'`. The source deliberately does not require p′ = p or require admission through that selected edge. `src/Program_Model/CFG/Collecting/Activation_Trace_Context.thy:89` defines admission by existence of a call edge, and `src/Program_Model/CFG/Collecting/Activation_Trace_Def.thy:120` permits a return using a call edge whose procedure matches the current result node. On an arbitrary graph, the procedure entered by the callee need not be the procedure named by its current result node.

   **Concrete counterexample to the printed reading.** Take a graph with entry u, two parameterless calls from u to procedures p and q, and distinct continuations kp and kq. Let the sole intra edge be a store-preserving edge from `FunctionEntry p` to `FunctionResult q`. Use the all-zero store, no globals, and no destination assignment, so every relevant transfer preserves that store. Admit context a for the p call and context b for the q call, with a ≠ b. Let the cover contain that store only at `(u,c0)`, `(FunctionEntry p,a)`, `(FunctionResult q,a)`, and `(FunctionEntry q,b)`. Init, Intra, Call, and Total hold. The printed Return tests `FunctionResult p` in a and `FunctionResult q` in b; both covers are empty, so Return holds vacuously. Nevertheless, the valid trace that enters p, crosses to q's result, and returns via the q edge reaches kq and resumes in c0, where the cover is empty. Theorem 1's printed interpretation fails. The stronger Isabelle Return obligation rejects this cover by admitting a through p′ = p while returning through q.

   This is a source-derived mathematical counterexample, **not an Isabelle-checked new lemma**. It exposes a mismatch between the displayed statement and the checked one, not an unsoundness of `activation_collect_sound`.

   **Required revision.** Either state the cross-edge Return obligation and explain why arbitrary graphs need it, or explicitly restrict the paper's simplified contract to graphs with the relevant call/ownership invariants and state the restricted theorem. Also make the return rule's call-edge premise explicit in Section 3.1: “the call edge” is not unique in the advertised arbitrary-graph setting. Do not leave the genericity claim and the same-edge simplification together.

## Minor comments

1. **Section 3.3, p. 6: give the strengthened induction invariant.** The paper says the invariant is that “every valid trace carries some context” and its store is covered in every such context. The actual induction maintains this for the entire caller chain: `src/Program_Model/CFG/Collecting/Activation_Trace_Abstract.thy:216` states `t ∈ T ==> (∀u ∈ callers t. trace_covered u)`. At lines 237–244, the return case extracts both the caller's and callee's coverage from that strengthened hypothesis. The immediate induction hypothesis for a return supplies the callee, so merely recovering the caller structurally does not itself supply the caller's coverage. One sentence about the caller-chain invariant would explain the actual hard step.

2. **Section 4.2, Theorem 2, p. 8: expose the important graph restriction.** “Side conditions on call resolution and the shape of the graph” conceals an assumption directly relevant to the main abstraction: `src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Context/Routed_Context.thy:74` requires `calls_unique: calls_source_unique g`. State this condition explicitly and distinguish the arbitrary-graph semantic theorem from its more restricted equation-system instantiation. This matters especially when resolving major comment 1; it is not evidence that the final compiled-program theorem lacks the assumption's discharge.

3. **Section 4.1 and Theorem 2, pp. 7–8: define the suppressed global environment.** The text uses unary γ and writes the cover as `γ(σ(v,c))`. The formal cover is `γ_DG (dg_local (sigma (Inl (v,c)))) (genv global_of sigma)`, as stated at `src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Activation/DG_Ctx_Activation.thy:50` and line 77. Moreover, `src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Spec/DG_Spec_Sound.thy:348` interprets transfer results against the environment joined with their publications. A short definition of γ relative to the final global valuation would connect the displayed theorem to the side-effecting semantics and explain why the side-effect bound in the solver certificate is needed. Theorem 2 also explicitly assumes `init_env_le: e0 ≤ genv global_of sigma` at `src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Context/Routed_Context.thy:589`; include this in the initialization explanation.

4. **Section 4.3, p. 8: qualify “Every node of a procedure reaches its result node.”** This is structural reachability using local edges and call-site-to-continuation shortcuts, not an executable path guaranteed to return. `src/Program_Model/Compile/Live_Nodes.thy:422` concludes `local_reaches`, and the explanation at line 9 explicitly includes those shortcuts. Add “in the local dependency graph, ignoring guards and treating calls as continuation edges.” Otherwise a reader may mistake this sentence for a termination or feasible-return claim.

5. **Figure 2 caption, p. 7: qualify independence.** “Each box is proved without reference to the others” is too absolute. The routing adequacy obligation explicitly invokes the analysis's enter operation, its concretization, and the solved valuation (`src/Abstract_Interpreter/Framework/Context/Routed_Context.thy:86`). Section 4.2 already explains this dependency correctly. Say the components are verified through explicit interfaces rather than claiming literal independence.

6. **Section 5, p. 10: make the adequacy conventions reproducible.** “Division by zero is defined” is accurate but underspecified. `src/Program_Model/VIMP/VIMP_Expr.thy:19` and line 23 define `a / 0 = 0` and `a % 0 = a`. Giving these two equations would let readers distinguish the source execution guarantee from the separately reported arithmetic-error guarantee. The distinction between unconstrained main locals and zeroed callee locals is correctly disclosed and should remain.
